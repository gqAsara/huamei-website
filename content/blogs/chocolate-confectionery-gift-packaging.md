# Chocolate and confectionery gift packaging: rigid box structures and food-safe considerations

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 19 May 2026. Updated 19 May 2026.

Sonia Sun has manufactured gift packaging for confectionery and food-adjacent gifting clients at Huamei since founding the company in Zhengzhou in 1992 — across rigid box, folding carton, and sleeve formats for seasonal gifting campaigns requiring transit-grade protection and food-contact-safe insert materials.

A chocolate gift box carries two obligations simultaneously: it must protect a fragile, temperature-sensitive product through transit, and it must communicate a gift's value within seconds of unwrapping. The structure, the insert, and the surface treatment each contribute to both functions. This guide covers the three main structures used for premium chocolate and confectionery packaging, the food-safe insert materials that meet international procurement standards, and the surface treatments that read as gift-quality at retail.

## What packaging structures are used for luxury chocolate gift boxes?

> Luxury chocolate gift boxes use three main structures: the rigid lift-off lid box (a two-piece greyboard box with a moulded or thermoformed insert tray holding individual pieces), the hinged rigid clamshell (lid and base joined by a spine, with a flocked or velvet interior tray), and the folding carton with insert (a 350–500 gsm printed board that collapses flat for shipping and holds a separate chocolate tray). Structure choice depends on price tier, transit distance, and whether the box is intended for retail display or gifting.

The [rigid lift-off lid](/craft/rigid) at 2.0–2.5 mm greyboard is the standard for premium chocolate gifting. The lid lifts free of the base to reveal the confectionery tray in a single motion. The greyboard core provides dimensional stability under the weight of the chocolates and the pressure of palletisation during shipping. At 2.0 mm greyboard, a 200 × 200 mm base holds a static load of 25–40 kg stacked vertically — adequate for retail shelf stacking but confirmed by empty-box compression testing before international freight.

The hinged rigid clamshell — a spine-joined lid and base — adds permanence to the box. The lid cannot be separated from the base and lost during gift presentation. For a high-price confectionery brand whose box is kept long after the chocolates are consumed, the clamshell format supports the box as a secondary object (a keepsake, a display piece, a storage box for small objects). The clamshell format adds tooling cost and production time relative to the lift-off lid, but produces a box that signals durability.

The [folding carton](/craft/folding) with a separate insert tray is the structure for brands that ship direct-to-consumer at volume. The folding carton collapses flat, reducing the packing cube during fulfilment; the insert tray — moulded pulp or thermoformed board — ships separately and is assembled by the fulfilment team. The folding carton allows a higher offset-print quality than a rigid box wrap (the board is printed before cutting, not wrapped after assembly), which suits designs with photographic imagery or fine-line illustration.

## What food-safe insert materials are used for chocolate packaging?

Food safety in chocolate packaging refers to materials that are in indirect contact with confectionery — the insert tray that holds each chocolate, not the box itself. Chocolate is enclosed in a paper or foil wrapper and does not directly contact the insert tray, but international retailers and brands with [BRCGS](https://www.brcgs.com/) food packaging certification require that all materials in the box meet food-safe grading.

Moulded pulp trays — formed from recycled or virgin paper fibre — are the standard food-safe insert for chocolate gift boxes. The moulded pulp is formed into cavities that hold each chocolate piece individually and is produced without adhesive or synthetic binder. Moulded pulp is [FSC](https://fsc.org/en)-certifiable, biodegradable, and meets BRCGS packaging safety requirements when produced from food-grade fibre. For brands with explicit sustainability requirements — European retailers often mandate both FSC chain-of-custody and a BRCGS-rated supplier — moulded pulp inserts are the correct specification.

Thermoformed clear-PET trays, produced from food-grade polyethylene terephthalate, are the alternative for brands that want a transparent display of the chocolate arrangement through the top of the box. The clear tray allows the colour and arrangement of the chocolates to be seen before the lid is removed, which is a retail and gifting presentation advantage. PET is recyclable as a single-material stream and meets food-contact safety standards across EU and US regulations.

Flocked card trays — a card substrate coated in a short-fibre velvet — are used for gifting tiers where the insert tray is part of the visual presentation rather than purely functional. The velvet surface reads as jewel-box quality; the card base is rigid enough to hold chocolates in place during transit. Flocked card is not a food-contact material and is used only where the chocolates are individually wrapped.

## What surface treatments communicate luxury on a confectionery gift box?

The confectionery gift box signals its value tier through the combination of wrap paper, foil, and finish. Three treatments are standard at the premium gift tier.

[Hot-foil stamping](/craft/hot-foil) on the lid in gold or champagne foil is the most legible premium signal in retail gifting. A single foil mark — a brand logotype, a holiday motif, a date — on a matt-coated wrap reads as considered restraint rather than decoration. Huamei holds seventeen foil colours and stamps at ±0.1 mm registration, which is tight enough for a fine-serif type treatment at 10-point size.

Embossing the wrap paper adds a tactile dimension to the foil mark — the brand logotype is pressed into the surface to a depth of 0.5–1.5 mm alongside the foil, creating a mark that is both seen and felt. For seasonal gifting — Chinese New Year, Mid-Autumn, Christmas — an emboss in a seasonal motif (blossom, moon, star) alongside the brand foil is the standard approach for [seasonal packaging](/industry/seasonal) at premium retail.

The [Man Made Crayon](/volumes/man-made-crayon) puzzle book-style box uses a kraft wrap with screen-printed single-colour and a blind emboss — the kraft surface and craft aesthetic suit a DTC gifting brand that communicates values through material choice. The [Pink Food Sleeve](/volumes/pink-food-sleeve) demonstrates how a hot-pink food sleeve — a folding sleeve format over a tray — can signal category at retail without a full rigid box at every price point.

## How to write a confectionery packaging brief

A complete confectionery packaging brief states: structure type (lift-off rigid / clamshell / folding carton), external dimensions (length × width × height in millimetres), number of chocolate cavities and individual piece dimensions, insert material (moulded pulp / thermoformed PET / flocked card), wrap paper type and colour, surface treatments (foil colour, emboss die reference, laminate), certification requirements (FSC, BSCI, BRCGS), and transit destination (domestic China / international air / international sea freight).
