# Gift box packaging for fashion and apparel brands: garments, scarves, and accessories

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 9 August 2026. Updated 9 August 2026.

Sonia Sun has built gift packaging for consumer goods brands across Huamei's thirty-four-year production history — from spirits and cosmetics to tea, wellness, and fashion. The fashion category arrives at a packaging brief with a set of structural and aesthetic challenges that differ meaningfully from the categories that dominate the luxury rigid box market: garments are soft, folded, and variable in thickness; the box must protect a textile surface without leaving marks; and the brand identity that works on a hang tag or a boutique bag must translate into paper, board, and foil.

A [rigid box](/craft/rigid) for a fashion gift context is not simply a decorated carton. It is the first physical encounter between a recipient and a garment they have not yet seen. The weight of the lid, the sound of the paper, the resistance of the ribbon pull-tab, and the moment the garment is revealed: these are designed experiences, not incidental ones.

## What rigid box structures are used for fashion and apparel gift packaging?

Fashion and apparel gift packaging uses three principal rigid box structures: the flat lift-off box for folded garments (shirts, knitwear, scarves), the magnetic closure book-style box for accessories and small leather goods, and the drawer-and-slipcase format for single premium items. Garment boxes run at 2.0–2.5 mm greyboard to prevent the lid from flexing under the weight of a folded garment; a recessed interior tray or ribbon pull-tab keeps the item in place on opening.

The flat lift-off is the dominant garment box format. The base is deep enough to hold a folded shirt at 50–80 mm interior depth; the lid is shallow (15–20 mm) and lifts off fully to expose the garment. Interior lining is typically a soft-touch paper or a velvet-style material — a texture that holds the garment without snagging — combined with a ribbon pull-tab that allows the item to be lifted without the buyer touching the textile directly.

The [magnetic closure](/craft/magnetic) book-style box is the format for accessories: a folded silk scarf, a small leather wallet, a cufflink set. The book-style opening — one face lifts as a lid from a magnetic closure on the long edge — is a reveal format. The interior of a book-style box for a scarf typically holds a card insert that keeps the folded scarf aligned, and a small ribbon loop at the base of the insert allows the scarf to be lifted out at the fold point.

"The flat lift-off rigid box is the dominant garment packaging format for fashion gifting: a 50–80 mm interior depth for a folded shirt or knitwear piece, a shallow 15–20 mm lid, and a ribbon pull-tab that lets the recipient lift the garment without direct contact. Greyboard at 2.0–2.5 mm prevents lid flex under garment weight."

## What interior specifications matter for garment and textile packaging?

A garment placed directly against a paper-lined interior will pick up surface debris from the paper during transport. For fashion packaging, the interior lining is selected to protect the textile surface: a smooth flocked paper, a thin fabric laminate, or a non-woven material bonded to the interior panel.

The interior lining choice also affects the foil and emboss programme on the interior face. A flocked interior does not accept hot-foil stamping cleanly; if the inside face of a lid or the base interior carries a logo or pattern in foil, a smooth paper or fabric laminate interior is the correct substrate. Interior [emboss](/craft/emboss) without foil is achievable on most smooth lining materials; the die presses through the lining into the greyboard below, producing a blind impression.

Ribbon pull-tabs in fashion garment boxes are typically 20 mm wide, in a satin or grosgrain fabric that matches or complements the exterior paper tone. The tab is bonded to the interior base at the centre and runs under the folded garment; when the recipient pulls the tab, the garment rises clear of the box. The ribbon colour and width are specified at the brief stage.

## What surface decoration translates a fashion brand identity into rigid box packaging?

Fashion brand identities tend toward either maximum restraint — a single monogram or wordmark in foil or blind deboss on a plain ground — or maximum surface richness — a repeat pattern across the full face, a bordered panel in a contrasting paper, or a die-cut exterior aperture. Both approaches have well-established production methods in luxury rigid box construction.

Restraint: a single wordmark in [hot-foil](/craft/hot-foil) on a coloured matte paper. The gold, silver, or tonal-match foil reads the brand mark clearly against the paper ground. The paper colour is the primary design element; the foil confirms the brand without competing with the textile inside. Huamei holds seventeen in-house foil colours, covering the full warm gold to cool silver range, with a set of tonal foils designed to read as low-contrast on matching paper grounds.

Surface richness: a full-face repeat pattern offset-printed in one or two colours on a coated stock, with a foil accent on the brand mark at the lid centre. This requires offset printing as a production step before assembly — the paper is printed flat, then applied to the greyboard. For a fashion seasonal gift box with a distinct pattern, offset printing allows the pattern to run to the paper edge without a visible print boundary.

[ISO 9001:2015](https://www.iso.org/iso-9001-quality-management.html) sample sign-off at Huamei requires written approval before production starts; for fashion programmes where the packaging colour must be confirmed against a physical textile sample, the sign-off process includes a colour-match check between the paper ground and the garment material. This is a step that benefits from the buyer sending a fabric swatch with the brief.

"Hot-foil on a plain matte paper ground is the standard fashion packaging decoration for restraint: a single wordmark at the lid centre, foil colour selected to read clearly against the paper ground without competing with the textile inside. Huamei's seventeen in-house foil colours cover tonal, warm gold, and cool silver ranges."

## MOQ and production considerations for fashion seasonal programmes

Fashion packaging programmes — a holiday gift box programme, a seasonal limited-edition collaboration, a store gifting assortment — tend to require larger assortments than spirits or cosmetics programmes. Multiple SKU sizes, multiple colourways for different markets, and mid-season replenishment orders are common. Huamei's MOQ floor is 200+ pieces per SKU; a four-SKU fashion gift box assortment runs at minimum 800 pieces across the range.

Sample requirements for fashion programmes typically include a physical textile sample in the brief, sent alongside the artwork file, so the paper and lining colour matching can be confirmed before production. The seven-to-ten day sample lead time applies from the date the textile sample arrives, not from the date the brief is submitted.

[FSC](https://fsc.org/en) certification is available for the board and paper components of fashion gift boxes — a requirement growing among European retail accounts and luxury e-commerce platforms. Fashion brands that commit to chain-of-custody documentation for their packaging should confirm this requirement at the brief stage.

The [Man Made Crayon case study](/volumes/man-made-crayon) demonstrates a gifting programme where box format, lining material, and exterior paper were confirmed together at the sample stage — the same process that applies to fashion gift box programmes where paper-to-textile colour harmony is the primary brief requirement.
