# Magnetic closure luxury boxes and international shipping regulations: what brands need to declare

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 9 August 2026. Updated 9 August 2026.

Sonia Sun has managed international shipping documentation for Huamei's export programmes since 1992, including magnetic closure rigid boxes destined for US, European, Middle Eastern, and Southeast Asian buyers. Questions about magnet regulations in air freight arise on nearly every magnetic closure programme shipped internationally — and the answer is consistently more nuanced than a single yes or no.

The [magnetic closure rigid box](/craft/magnetic) is one of the highest-specified formats in luxury gifting. A closure pull-force from 6 to 50 g at 2,800 Gauss provides the tactile resistance that premium packaging requires. The question international buyers ask — and should ask before confirming sea versus air freight — is whether that magnet strength triggers any freight declaration requirement.

## Do magnetic closure luxury boxes require air freight declaration?

Magnetic material requires air freight declaration under [IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations (DGR)](https://www.iata.org/en/programs/cargo/dgr/) Packing Instruction 953 when the magnetic field strength at 2.1 metres from the package exceeds 0.418 A/m. Most luxury rigid box closures at 2,800 Gauss — including Huamei's full closure range — do not trigger this threshold when the box is properly closed and the magnets are fully enclosed in greyboard. Confirmation requires a magnetometer measurement of the finished packed unit, not a calculation from magnet strength alone.

The key variable is not the magnet's rated Gauss figure but the external field at a specified distance from the packaged unit. A magnet rated at 2,800 Gauss as a component produces a different external field than that same magnet enclosed in greyboard, wrapped in paper, packed in a polybag, and placed inside a master carton. The greyboard and paper layers attenuate the external field significantly; in practice, the standard luxury rigid box with embedded N35-grade neodymium magnets — the grade most commonly used in 6–35 g pull-force closures — falls below the IATA threshold in finished-pack configuration.

"Under IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations Packing Instruction 953, magnetized material requires declaration when the external field at 2.1 m exceeds 0.418 A/m. Most magnetic closure rigid boxes in finished-pack configuration — greyboard, paper wrap, polybag, master carton — fall below this threshold, but measurement of the actual packed unit is required to confirm."

## What drives the threshold: field strength versus magnet rating

Two concepts that are frequently confused in buyer conversations: Gauss, which is the unit of magnetic flux density internal to the magnet itself, and the external field at distance, which is what IATA regulates. A 2,800 Gauss magnet is a material specification, not a shipping classification.

The [ICAO Technical Instructions](https://www.icao.int/safety/DangerousGoods/Pages/technical-instructions.aspx) define the test: the magnetometer is placed 2.1 m from the surface of the packaged unit. At that distance, the external field of a correctly enclosed magnetic closure box typically falls below the 0.418 A/m threshold — because the magnetic field of a flat-disc or bar magnet attenuates as the cube of the distance from the source. At 2.1 m from a package holding standard N35 magnets in greyboard construction, the field is reduced by several orders of magnitude from the surface field of the bare magnet component.

The exceptions — packages where the threshold is approached or exceeded — tend to involve industrial-grade magnets at N52 or stronger, magnetic assemblies without greyboard shielding, or unusually high concentrations of magnets in a single package (more than sixty magnetic closure boxes per master carton without interlayer padding). None of these conditions describe standard luxury rigid box programmes.

## How Huamei's transit-grade testing addresses magnetic compliance

Huamei's transit-grade testing programme includes environmental simulation at high 50 °C and low -30 °C, 24-hour vibration transit simulation, drop testing, aging testing, and empty-box compression testing. These tests address structural integrity across the full export supply chain. For magnetic closure programmes destined for air freight, Huamei provides a magnetometer reading of the finished master-carton configuration on request, confirming the external field at 2.1 m for airline cargo documentation.

This measurement is performed on the actual production pack configuration — box closed, polybag sealed, master carton packed at the standard layer count. It is not a calculation from component specs. International [BSCI](https://www.bsci-intl.org/)-audited factories are required to document their export compliance practices; Huamei holds BSCI, CE, EQS, [FSC](https://fsc.org/en), and SGS certifications and produces the magnetic closure compliance measurement as part of the standard export documentation package.

"Huamei provides magnetometer readings of magnetic closure boxes in finished master-carton configuration on request, confirming compliance with IATA Packing Instruction 953 thresholds for airline cargo documentation — a requirement that applies to any programme shipping by air to US or European destinations."

## Sea freight versus air freight for magnetic closure programmes

Sea freight does not carry the IATA magnetized material classification. A magnetic closure rigid box programme shipped by sea from China to a US or European port does not require any magnet-specific declaration under IMO / IMDG regulations, provided the packages are correctly declared by product category (the finished goods, not a magnetic component).

Air freight is where the classification question matters. For time-sensitive luxury programmes — holiday launches that must arrive within a narrow window, replenishment orders for retail accounts — air freight is sometimes the default. For these shipments, the magnetometer confirmation should be in hand before booking freight. Most forwarders handling luxury goods routinely request confirmation for any magnetic closure programme; having the measurement in the export file avoids delays at origin or destination.

The [Wuliangye premium spirits clamshell case study](/volumes/wuliangye-clamshell) demonstrates a complex rigid format shipped internationally, with export documentation confirming structural and compliance standards. The same documentation framework applies to magnetic closure programmes.

## What to request at the brief stage for international magnetic closure programmes

A buyer placing an international magnetic closure order should request three things at the brief confirmation stage: the pull-force specification for the selected closure (in grams, at the selected Gauss rating), the N-grade of the magnet component (to confirm the category), and the magnetometer measurement for the proposed master-carton pack configuration.

The [rigid box](/craft/rigid) brief template at Huamei includes a field for intended freight mode (sea / air / both); when air is indicated or possible, the magnetometer measurement is scheduled as part of the sample confirmation — not as an afterthought before shipping.
