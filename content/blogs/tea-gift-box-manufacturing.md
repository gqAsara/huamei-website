# Tea gift box manufacturing: structures, finishes, and what buyers should specify

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 17 May 2026. Updated 17 May 2026.

Sonia Sun has manufactured packaging for tea brands at Huamei since founding the company in Zhengzhou in 1992 — including work for [DEEPURE](/volumes/heritage-tea) and [T2 True Brews](/volumes/t2-tea), two very different formats on opposite ends of the tea gift-box spectrum.

Tea packaging occupies an unusual position in luxury packaging: the product inside is light, dry, and relatively tolerant of structural variation, but the presentation standard is set by the occasion — gifting, retail display, export — and the structural decisions flow from that. A monocarton is sufficient for a teabag retail stack; a rigid drawer box with a gold-foil leaf motif and a satin ribbon pull is appropriate for a single-origin loose-leaf collector set. Understanding where a brief sits on that spectrum is the first decision in tea gift box manufacturing.

## What structures are most common for premium tea gift boxes?

> The three most common structures for premium tea gift boxes are: the sleeve-and-tray (a sliding tray inside a printed sleeve, suited to sachets and single-serve packets), the drawer box (a hand-pulled interior within a rigid outer shell, suited to loose-leaf tins or pouch sets), and the magnetic-flap rigid box (a hinged lid secured by embedded magnets, suited to collector tins and multi-SKU gift sets).

Each structure has a different cost profile. At Huamei's 200-piece MOQ floor, the sleeve-and-tray is the most accessible entry point: no magnets, no additional die for a recessed lid, low tooling cost. The drawer box steps up in cost because the ribbon pull and the slide mechanism require more hand-assembly time per unit — hand-assembly is the primary cost driver in true luxury packaging, not materials. The magnetic-flap rigid box adds the cost of embedded neodymium magnets (pull-force calibrated between 6 and 50 grams at 2,800 Gauss, depending on lid weight and usage frequency) and the deeper structural work of a full-wrap [rigid box](/craft/rigid) construction.

## How does surface decoration work on tea packaging?

Surface decoration on tea packaging serves two distinct purposes: brand identification and occasion-signalling. A supermarket tea is identified; a gift tea is signalled.

[Hot-foil stamping](/craft/hot-foil) is the most common premium finish on tea gift boxes. The foil die is engraved to the artwork — a leaf motif, a calligraphic brand name, a border rule — and the foil is pressed at heat and pressure against the paper wrap. Huamei holds seventeen curated foil colours in-house, from traditional gold and silver through matte blacks and iridescent options. Foil registration against a printed element or an emboss is held to ±0.1 mm, which is three times tighter than the industry-typical ±0.3 mm tolerance and critical when a gold leaf motif must land precisely inside a debossed border.

The [DEEPURE Heritage Tea](/volumes/heritage-tea) case uses a red base wrap with a gold-foil leaf motif — the structure is a rigid magnetic box, and the foil work runs registered to an embossed panel on the lid surface. The [T2 True Brews](/volumes/t2-tea) brief took a different direction: a multi-colour sleeve format with a high-saturation printed palette, no foil, with the decoration doing the occasion-signalling through colour rather than material finish. Both approaches are correct for their respective brand registers.

## What paper and board should a tea gift box specify?

A tea gift box brief specifies two distinct material layers: the structural board and the wrap paper.

The structural board for a rigid tea box sits between 1.5 mm and 3.0 mm of greyboard. A 2.0 mm greyboard is the standard for a small-to-medium gift box (a single canister or a pouch set). A 2.5 mm or 3.0 mm greyboard is appropriate for a large collector set with multiple tins or a presentation case with a foam insert — the heavier board prevents lid sag and provides the structural rigidity that makes a magnetic closure land cleanly.

The wrap paper is the tactile and visual surface. Tea packaging uses a wide range of wraps: uncoated kraft for a natural-origins register, coated gloss or matte for a clean, modern luxury feel, embossable specialty papers (from mills including [Fedrigoni](https://www.fedrigoni.com/) and [Gmund](https://www.gmund.com/)) for a tactile surface that responds to emboss and deboss. Soft-touch lamination — a polyurethane topcoat that adds a velvet-dry hand feel — is an increasingly common choice on tea packaging aimed at European and North American retail, where the tactile first impression at gift-giving matters as much as the visual one.

[FSC](https://fsc.org/en) chain-of-custody certification is available on Huamei's board and wrap sourcing, meeting the requirement for European brands with forest-responsibility commitments.

## What should a tea packaging brief include?

A complete tea gift box brief covers six elements. Missing any one typically adds at least one revision round to the sample stage.

**1. Structure type.** Sleeve-and-tray, drawer, or magnetic-flap rigid box. If unknown, share the SKU count and product weight — the factory can recommend.

**2. Interior contents.** What goes inside: tea tins, pouches, sachets, single-serve sticks, accessories. Dimensions and weight of each item determine cavity sizing and insert type.

**3. Board weight and wrap paper.** Or a reference sample from an existing pack the brand wants to match in quality register. The sample round confirms the actual thickness.

**4. Decoration intent.** Foil colour and placement, emboss/deboss locations, print colour count, any special finishes (soft-touch, spot-UV, matte lamination). Foil and emboss elements need artwork files at vector resolution.

**5. Volume and in-market date.** Volume determines whether a tooled insert (moulded pulp, foam die) amortises. The in-market date sets the production calendar: 7–10 days for sample, 15–20 days for production run, plus freight and customs.

**6. Certification requirements.** [FSC](https://fsc.org/en) chain-of-custody, [BSCI](https://www.amfori.org/content/amfori-bsci) audit compliance, [SGS](https://www.sgs.com/) or equivalent test reports — state upfront which certifications the purchase order will require. Huamei holds BSCI, CE, EQS, FSC, and SGS.

[ISO 9001:2015](https://www.iso.org/iso-9001-quality-management.html) quality management governs the production process across all four Huamei factories.
