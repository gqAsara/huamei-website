# Custom wedding favour packaging: structures, finishes, and MOQ from 200 pieces

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 10 August 2026. Updated 10 August 2026.

Sonia Sun has run the packaging floor at Huamei since founding the company in 1992, overseeing gifting programmes across seasonal, corporate, and personal occasions — including favour gifting for high-end event clients where each unit represents a guest's first physical interaction with a brand or a couple's identity.

Wedding favour packaging occupies an unusual space in the production calendar: the quantities are gifting-scale rather than retail-scale, the personalisation requirements are high, and the delivery window is fixed by a date that cannot move. A favour box that arrives two weeks after the wedding date is worthless. The brief timeline therefore governs everything — structure, finish, and decoration decisions all flow from how many production weeks are available between artwork approval and the event.

## What is the minimum order quantity for custom wedding favour boxes?

The public MOQ floor for custom wedding favour boxes at Huamei is 200+ pieces — sufficient for mid-size and large guest lists. Sample lead time is 7–10 days; production runs 15–20 days. The total timeline from brief to shipped production is 22–30 working days.

For a wedding guest list of 200–500, the 200-piece MOQ makes a fully custom rigid box programme viable without requiring retail-scale quantities. Guest lists above 500 scale into the range where further unit cost reductions become available, though MOQ pricing is not published; a direct brief through [/begin](/begin) returns a programme-specific quotation.

## Which rigid box structures suit wedding favour packaging?

A [rigid box](/craft/rigid) in a small footprint — typically 100–150 mm on the longest face — is the most common structure for a premium wedding favour. The four structures most requested for favour gifting are the two-piece set box, the single-piece magnetic closure box, the book-style hinged box, and the sleeve-and-tray.

The two-piece set box (lid and base, no closure mechanism) is the simplest and lowest cost per unit. It works well when the favour is displayed on tables before guests arrive — the box presents cleanly closed and lifts without a mechanism. The single-piece [magnetic closure](/craft/magnetic) box adds a pull-force of 6–50 grams at 2,800 Gauss depending on the closure type and board weight selected; it is preferred when the favour is handed out at the event because the closure keeps it secure during handling without requiring a ribbon or tape seal. The book-style hinged box creates an opening experience — the cover swings away to reveal the contents — and is appropriate when the favour itself is display-worthy and the box is part of the presentation. The sleeve-and-tray is the most economical of the four for high-volume programmes; the tray slides out from the printed sleeve, keeping decoration on one printed surface while reducing the total board area.

"A single-piece magnetic closure box at 6–50 grams pull-force keeps a wedding favour sealed during table-setting and guest handling without requiring a ribbon or adhesive — the correct choice when the favour is distributed, not pre-placed."

## What surface finishes work for wedding favour packaging?

Wedding favour packaging most often calls for a restrained finish palette: a single foil colour, a blind deboss or emboss, and a matte or soft-touch lamination. These three elements in combination read as premium without competing with the favour product inside.

[Hot-foil stamping](/craft/hot-foil) in gold, silver, or rose gold is the decoration most associated with wedding occasion packaging. Huamei holds seventeen curated foil colours in-house, including warm golds, cool silvers, and pink-toned rose golds that align with standard wedding colour palettes. For a monogrammed initial or a floral motif, hot-foil on a matte-laminated ground produces a result where the metallic sits clearly against the flat surface without glare competing against the foil's lustre.

[Blind emboss or deboss](/craft/emboss) — a raised or recessed impression without ink or foil — adds a tactile element that reads well when the favour box is lifted. A debossed name or crest on a soft-touch laminated lid is a decoration choice that photographs clearly and feels distinct at handling. The combined foil-and-emboss registration at Huamei is held to ±0.1 mm, meaning an embossed border and a foil-filled panel inside it can be executed without visible misalignment.

Matte and soft-touch laminations both suppress surface sheen and increase scratch resistance. Soft-touch adds a velvety tactile response to the laminated surface; matte is smoother. Either is appropriate for favour packaging where the box is handled multiple times before the guest opens it.

For couples whose event literature emphasises sustainability, the wrapping paper can be specified from [FSC](https://fsc.org/en)-certified stocks — Huamei holds eighty papers on file, including FSC-certified options across both uncoated specialty and standard art papers.

## How is a personalised name or detail added to favour packaging?

Personalisation on rigid box packaging takes two forms: programme-level personalisation (a shared design with the couple's name and date) and individual-level personalisation (a different name on each box). Programme-level personalisation is straightforward — the artwork is a single file, run as one production job. Individual-level personalisation, where each of 300 guests receives a box with their own name on it, requires either a variable-data print process (digital printing on the wrap paper before box construction) or a secondary operation such as letterpress or engraved insert card.

Most wedding favour programmes at this scale use programme-level personalisation for the box exterior and individual-level personalisation on an insert card inside — the card carrying the guest's name in a printed or handwritten format. This keeps the rigid box production clean and standard while the insert handles the guest-specific element.

The [Man Made Crayon case study](/volumes/man-made-crayon) demonstrates a gifting programme where a single consistent exterior structure held differentiated interior contents — the production logic that applies to a favour programme where the box is uniform and the insert varies.

## What is the production timeline for custom wedding favour boxes?

A complete custom wedding favour box programme — brief received, artwork confirmed, sample approved, production released — runs 22–30 working days at Huamei. The sample phase alone is 7–10 days; production runs 15–20 days following sample approval.

For events with a fixed date, the brief calendar working backwards: if the wedding is on a Saturday, and sea freight transit from China to the destination country adds 25–35 days, the production release window at the factory closes approximately eight weeks before the event date. Air freight shortens the transit to 5–10 days and adds flexibility, at a per-unit cost that is typically 3–5 times sea freight for comparable volumes.

The most consistent cause of schedule compression in favour programmes is late artwork sign-off. A brief that arrives with confirmed artwork, confirmed paper, and confirmed foil reference can move to sample immediately; a brief that arrives with design decisions still open adds time at each stage. The 22–30 day production figure assumes a clean brief — artwork locked, no further colour matching required.
