# Vegan and clean beauty packaging: rigid box specification for cruelty-free and B Corp cosmetic brands

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 11 August 2026. Updated 11 August 2026.

Sonia Sun has produced luxury rigid box packaging for cosmetic and wellness brands at Huamei since 1992, including skincare programmes where supply-chain transparency and material traceability are the primary procurement criteria. The clean beauty and vegan beauty segments have each developed distinct supplier audit requirements — and the packaging box has moved from a secondary concern to a primary audit checkpoint in many of these programmes.

The question "is this packaging vegan?" arrives in briefs with increasing frequency from US and UK brands, particularly those holding B Corp certification, targeting Whole Foods Market, or aligning with [The Vegan Society](https://www.vegansociety.com/) Vegan Trademark standard. The answer requires knowing which conventional packaging materials contain animal derivatives, which substitutes are readily available, and how to document the material chain in a way that satisfies a retail buyer's supplier audit questionnaire.

## What does vegan packaging mean for a luxury rigid box?

Vegan packaging for a luxury rigid box means the absence of animal-derived materials at every stage: no shellac in surface lacquers, no casein (milk protein) in adhesive coatings, no silk fibres in specialty wrapping papers, and no bone-char carbon black in conventional offset inks. Most standard rigid box materials — greyboard, FSC-certified paper, metallic foil film, water-based adhesives, and soy-blend inks — are vegan-compliant.

The gap between "conventional luxury packaging" and "vegan luxury packaging" is smaller than many brands assume when they first raise the question. The most common animal-derived material in premium packaging — shellac in spirit varnish — has already been largely replaced by UV varnishes and water-based lacquers in standard production. Casein-based adhesives, once common in paper manufacturing, have similarly been replaced in most contemporary paper stocks by synthetic binders. Bone-char-derived carbon black is the least visible risk: it persists in some conventional offset ink formulations as a carbon black pigment, and requires explicit confirmation from the ink manufacturer.

"Vegan packaging for a luxury rigid box requires confirming the absence of shellac in surface lacquers, casein in adhesive coatings, silk fibres in wrapping paper, and bone-char-derived carbon black in offset inks. Standard rigid box materials — greyboard, FSC paper, metallic foil film, water-based adhesives — are vegan-compliant without modification."

## Which conventional packaging materials conflict with vegan or clean beauty requirements?

Four material categories warrant confirmation in a vegan packaging brief:

**Shellac in surface varnish.** Shellac is a resin secreted by the lac beetle and was historically used in spirit varnishes for its hardness and gloss. Contemporary luxury packaging production has largely moved to UV-cured and water-based lacquer systems; confirm with the factory that no shellac-based coatings are in the production workflow.

**Casein in paper coatings.** Casein (milk protein) was used as a coating binder in some coated art papers. Most contemporary mill-produced specialty papers have moved to synthetic latex binders for coating adhesion; confirm the binder type with the mill's technical data sheet for any specialty stock specified in the brief — particularly for handmade or artisan papers where traditional binder recipes may still be in use.

**Silk fibres in specialty wrapping papers.** Some premium Japanese and European handmade papers incorporate silk or other animal fibre; these are occasionally specified for luxury wrapping. Standard machine-made specialty papers in Huamei's eighty-paper wrapping library do not incorporate animal fibres.

**Bone-char carbon black in offset inks.** Carbon black is used as the black pigment in offset inks. Some carbon black is produced through bone char (charred animal bones); synthetic carbon black produced from petroleum-derived feedstocks is the industry standard, but the distinction must be confirmed with the ink manufacturer. Soy-blend inks at Huamei use synthetic carbon black; the material data sheet (MDS) for the ink confirms the pigment origin.

## What materials and inks can a vegan-compliant rigid box use?

Standard [rigid box](/craft/rigid) construction materials are vegan-compliant: chipboard and greyboard cores use cellulose-based binders; wrapping papers are wood-pulp-based; water-based PVA adhesives used in assembly are synthetic; and metalised polyester foil films — the carrier for [hot-foil stamping](/craft/hot-foil) — contain no animal-derived components.

For [offset printing](/craft/offset), Huamei holds soy-blend inks on file for select production runs. Soy-blend inks substitute a portion of the petroleum-derived ink vehicle with soy-derived oil, reducing VOC (volatile organic compound) output and improving ink biodegradability compared to petroleum-based inks. They are confirmed vegan-compliant through the ink manufacturer's material data sheet. A soy-blend ink specification is noted on the production sheet and does not affect the press colour output measurably on Huamei's Heidelberg and KBA press systems.

Hot-foil films are metalised polyester — no animal-derived components. The seventeen curated foil colours in Huamei's in-house library are all metalised carrier films; the foil selection step in a vegan brief does not require any modification from a standard programme.

"Metallic hot-foil films used in luxury packaging are metalised polyester — no animal-derived materials. Soy-blend offset inks are available at Huamei for select runs, confirmed vegan-compliant through the ink manufacturer's material data sheet. Foil and soy-ink programmes require no structural changes relative to a standard brief."

## Does FSC certification satisfy the sustainability requirements of clean beauty brands?

[FSC chain-of-custody certification](https://fsc.org/en) is the single most commonly specified paper and board sustainability credential in clean beauty brand packaging audits. It confirms that the paper and board used in the packaging comes from responsibly managed forests — covering deforestation, biodiversity, and local community rights. Huamei holds FSC chain-of-custody certification; the documentation package includes the FSC certificate and transaction verification.

FSC does not by itself confirm vegan compliance — FSC is a forest-management and chain-of-custody standard, not a material-contents standard. A vegan brief requires FSC certification plus the specific material confirmations listed above. B Corp certification standards and many US retailer supplier codes distinguish between sustainability credentials (FSC, BSCI) and material-compliance credentials (vegan, cruelty-free); both categories need to be addressed in the supplier documentation package.

Huamei holds BSCI, CE, EQS, FSC, and SGS certifications on file. These cover the social-responsibility and environmental audit requirements most commonly encountered in US and EU clean beauty procurement. The Huamei factory energy system runs at over 80% solar generation — the green energy share is relevant for brands calculating their Scope 3 emissions from packaging manufacture.

"Huamei holds FSC chain-of-custody, BSCI, CE, EQS, and SGS certifications on file. Over 80% of factory energy comes from solar generation. These credentials cover the most common sustainability audit requirements from US and EU clean beauty brands — FSC for paper sourcing, BSCI for social compliance, solar energy for Scope 3 reduction."

## How do you document vegan packaging compliance for retail and brand audit?

Documentation for a vegan packaging compliance package typically requires: a material list for all components (board, wrapping paper, adhesive, ink, foil, insert), the manufacturer's material data sheet (MDS) for each material confirming the absence of animal-derived ingredients, FSC chain-of-custody certificates for paper and board, and the ink manufacturer's declaration on carbon black source.

Huamei provides the material list and FSC documentation as part of the standard documentation package. Ink MDS documents are available from the ink manufacturer and are provided on request. For programmes targeting The Vegan Society Vegan Trademark, the brand applies for the trademark directly and Huamei provides supplier documentation to support the application.

The [Collgene case](/volumes/collgene) illustrates the documentation depth achievable for a skincare brand with international retail ambitions: the programme included full material traceability documentation alongside FSC and BSCI certification records. The [Kefumei case](/volumes/kefumei) shows a parallel-SKU cosmetic programme where material consistency across mass and premium lines was confirmed through a shared documentation set.
