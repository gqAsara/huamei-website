# Folding gift box: structures, board weights, and when to choose folding over rigid

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 26 May 2026. Updated 26 May 2026.

Sonia Sun has specified folding and rigid gift box structures across ninety-nine format variants since founding Huamei in 1992 — producing both collapsible folding-carton and fixed rigid-greyboard formats across four factories in Henan, Zhejiang, Sichuan, and Guizhou.

The term "folding gift box" covers a wide range of structures: the kraft tuck box that holds a candle, the auto-lock base box under a cosmetics set lid, and the collapsible rigid box that ships flat and assembles in the DC before a seasonal launch. What they share is scored, folded paperboard as the structural material — as opposed to the laminated greyboard core of a true rigid box. The choice between folding and rigid is a structural decision with downstream cost, logistics, and brand experience implications. This guide explains where the line sits.

## What is a folding gift box?

> A folding gift box is a gift box made from scored and folded paperboard that collapses flat for storage and shipping, then assembles by hand or machine at the point of use. Board weight ranges from 300 to 500 gsm, making it lighter and less expensive than a rigid greyboard box, but less structurally permanent.

Folding cartons — the category that includes most gift boxes in the 300–500 gsm range — are produced from a single flat board that is printed, laminated, die-cut, and scored, then glued at one seam to create a tube that collapses flat. The buyer or retailer opens the flat carton, folds the base and lid into shape, and uses it. No secondary assembly step at the factory is needed; this is what makes folding cartons faster and less expensive to produce at volume than rigid boxes.

The structural ceiling of a folding gift box is the point at which the paperboard walls begin to distort under product weight or stacking load. For lightweight goods — candles, small cosmetics, tea sachets, confectionery — a 400 gsm folding carton is fully adequate. For heavier products — glass spirits bottles, ceramic vases, or electronics — the folding carton's walls will not hold without an additional corrugated shipper, and a rigid box is the correct choice.

## When is a folding gift box the right choice?

A folding gift box is the right choice when the product is light enough for 300–500 gsm board walls, when logistics costs are driven by carton cube (folding cartons ship flat, cutting volume substantially), and when the brand budget does not require the weight and permanence of a rigid greyboard box.

The typical use cases where a folding gift box is structurally and commercially correct:

- **Candles and home fragrance:** 200–500 g products. A 400 gsm folding carton with auto-lock base and tuck-lid provides adequate support and photographs well with emboss and foil decoration.
- **Tea, confectionery, and small food gifting:** lightweight products that ship in volume. The [Glees Grove](/volumes/glees-grove) packaging format illustrates how a collapsible structure can carry sophisticated decoration in a flat-ship form.
- **Cosmetics accessory sets:** brushes, accessories, travel-size products. The weight is low; the unboxing is a retail moment, not a collector experience.
- **Consumer goods gifting:** the [Man Made Crayon](/volumes/man-made-crayon) format is an example of a gift box built from folded paperboard that carries strong decoration and brand identity at a lower structural cost than rigid.

When the product is a premium spirits bottle, a fragrance bottle above 100 ml, or any item where the packaging itself signals investment and permanence, a rigid greyboard box is the correct choice. The buyer's hand-feel at the moment of opening tells them which tier they are in; a folding carton cannot replicate the weight and corner rigidity of 2.0–3.0 mm greyboard.

## What board weights are used in folding gift boxes?

Board weight determines the fold quality, the emboss depth possible, and the structural performance under load.

- **300 gsm:** the lightest commercial grade. Used for gift-wrap boxes, printed sleeve sleeves, and lightweight cartons. Limited emboss depth; foil adhesion adequate.
- **350 gsm:** the standard gift-carton weight. Holds a clean corner fold; accepts hot-foil and emboss without surface cracking at the score line.
- **400 gsm:** the heavy grade for gift boxes carrying product up to 500 g. Corner fold is clean; the box self-supports in display conditions.
- **500 gsm:** specialty heavyweight. Used for auto-lock base structures under heavy cosmetics or gifting sets. Approaches the lower end of rigid-box performance; more expensive per unit than thinner grades.

Eighty paper substrates are on file at Huamei's factories, covering uncoated natural stocks, coated gloss and matte grades, specialty surface papers including pearlescent and linen-texture, and [FSC](https://fsc.org/en)-certified papers for sustainable-packaging programmes.

## What surface finishes apply to folding gift boxes?

Folding gift boxes accept the same surface finishes as rigid boxes — the difference is in depth limits. Because the paperboard core of a folding carton is thinner than a greyboard core, emboss depth is shallower: typically 0.3–0.5 mm on a 400 gsm board versus 0.8–1.2 mm on 2.0 mm greyboard.

Standard finishes:

- **Hot-foil stamping:** seventeen curated colours, in-house. Applied on the outer face, spine, or lid panel.
- **Emboss:** brand mark relief on the lid. Shallower than rigid-box emboss but fully readable at 0.3–0.5 mm.
- **Soft-touch laminate:** matte velvet surface. Popular on mid-market gift boxes.
- **Spot UV:** gloss accent on matte laminate. Low-cost, high-impact on gift retail.
- **Full-coverage gloss or matte laminate:** the base layer on most printed gift cartons; provides abrasion resistance and colour saturation.

## How do I order custom folding gift boxes?

Custom folding gift boxes from Huamei start at 200+ pieces MOQ. The process:

1. **Brief:** exterior dimensions (L × W × H flat), board weight, structural format (tuck lid, auto-lock base, sleeve, or collapsible rigid), surface finish.
2. **Die-line proof:** a flat die-line drawing, confirmed by both parties before production of the cutting die.
3. **Sample:** 7–10 days from approved brief to a physical sample. Folds, corners, and finish are confirmed on the sample.
4. **Production:** 15–20 days from approved sample to sealed master carton.
