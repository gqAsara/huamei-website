# Press Kit Box Packaging: How to Spec a Launch Box for PR and Trade Presentations

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 29 July 2026. Updated 29 July 2026.

Sonia Sun has run Huamei's press floor since founding the company in Zhengzhou in 1992 — more than three decades building launch packaging for brands across cosmetics, spirits, wellness, and consumer goods.

A press kit box is the brand's first physical impression on a journalist, a buyer, or a trade partner. It arrives by courier — not on a retail shelf — and must survive the post room, the unboxing, and the photograph that appears in a product feature or a brand review. The brief is different from a standard retail box in one critical way: the box must perform as both packaging and presentation in the same moment. There is no outer carton. There is no retail context to explain it. The box is the message. For general rigid box principles, see [custom luxury rigid box manufacturing](/blogs/custom-luxury-rigid-box-manufacturing).

## What is a press kit box and how does it differ from a retail gift box?

A press kit box is a custom rigid enclosure designed to deliver a brand's new product to media, buyers, or trade partners — built to open dramatically, hold a curated object set, and survive courier or postal delivery without external cartons.

The difference from a retail gift box is structural. A retail gift box lives in a display environment; it needs to stack, face outward, and read from two metres at shelf height. A press kit box lives inside a courier envelope or a DHL bag; it needs to survive a drop from a post-room conveyor, maintain its shape under the weight of three other parcels on top, and open cleanly when it finally reaches the desk of an editor in a New York or London office.

Three structural implications follow from that difference:

1. The outer box must be rigid enough to protect contents without a secondary carton. That means a minimum of 2.0 mm greyboard on the base — 2.5 mm if the set contains glass.
2. The closure must be positive — a magnetic flap at 18–25 g pull-force, not a loose tuck-end — so the box arrives closed, not spilled.
3. The interior must hold products in position without foam blocks protruding or shifting in transit.

## What structures work best for a press kit box?

A magnetic-flap book-style rigid box on 2.0 mm greyboard, with a layered foam or thermoformed insert, is the most versatile structure for a press kit because it opens flat on a desk, displays contents at a single level, and closes securely for courier delivery.

Three structures cover the majority of press kit briefs:

**Magnetic-flap book-style.** The lid swings open on a spine, revealing the full product array at once. Products sit in a custom [EVA foam or thermoformed insert](/craft/inserts) cut to the exact product silhouettes. The unboxing is a single gesture: open, see everything. This is the dominant format for cosmetics and skincare launches. The [Lavender Orchid case study](/volumes/lavender-orchid) shows what a fully finished cosmetics launch box looks like at production scale.

**Magnetic-flap two-piece with ribbon pull.** A standard two-piece rigid box [with a magnetic closure](/craft/magnetic) and a ribbon tab on the lid interior. The recipient lifts the ribbon to open. Slightly slower to unbox than the book-style, which means the contents remain hidden for an extra moment — useful for brands that want to build anticipation before the reveal.

**Drawer-in-sleeve.** A sleeve-wrapped outer with a sliding inner drawer. Clean shelf presence, easy to display in a trade show environment, and the drawer gesture is distinctive enough to be photographed in isolation. Less common for courier delivery because the sleeve can telescope if the courier handler turns it upside down.

For most press kit applications, the magnetic-flap book-style is the right choice. It photographs well, unboxes quickly, and can accommodate product sets from two to twelve items depending on the insert geometry.

## How do you brief a press kit packaging project?

A press kit packaging brief has seven required elements: outer dimensions, inner product list with weights and heights, closure preference, wrap material, surface finish combination, quantity, and the in-hand date — in that order.

The most common cause of brief delay is a missing product list. The insert geometry is the first thing the press floor calculates; without a complete product list (including every item in the set, with actual dimensions and weights, not approximate), the sample cannot be cut. "Approximate" dimensions become a problem when the margin is tight — a product that is 3 mm taller than stated will not close under the lid.

"A press kit box brief that arrives with a complete product list, confirmed wrap material, and surface finish direction takes 7–10 days to sample; one that requires three rounds of clarification takes three times as long."

Surface finish direction matters more for press kit boxes than for retail gift boxes, because the box is going to appear in photographs. A matte-laminated wrap with spot-UV on the brand mark photographs cleaner than a glossy wrap under mixed editorial lighting. Hot-foil catches light in a way that reads expensive in photography. We can advise on finish combinations based on the shoot environment and the recipient market, but the brief must at least specify a direction.

A full brief guide is available at [how to write a packaging brief](/blogs/how-to-write-packaging-brief).

## What is the right MOQ and lead time for a press kit box?

Press kit boxes are available at 200 pieces minimum; a sample runs 7–10 days from approved brief, and a production run ships in 15–20 days from sample sign-off.

"Huamei press kit boxes start at 200 pieces — making a launch run commercially viable without the excess inventory risk of a retail box order."

The minimum order of 200 pieces suits most press and trade launch runs. A new product introduction (NPI) going to 50 editors, 50 trade buyers, and 100 influencers lands precisely at 200. Brands wanting to retain reserve inventory for follow-up outreach typically order 250–300.

[ISO 9001:2015](https://www.iso.org/iso-9001-quality-management.html) quality management is the framework behind the QC procedure on every press kit run. Each box in the production lot is checked for closure alignment, insert fit, wrap tension, and surface finish quality before packing.

Lead time calculation for a product launch: if the in-hand date is 12 weeks out, the schedule has room for two sample rounds and a comfortable production window. If the in-hand date is 5 weeks out, the brief must arrive complete on day one — no revisions — to make it on ocean freight. Under 5 weeks, the conversation becomes one about air freight.

For broader context on MOQ and batch-size decisions, see [MOQ realities in luxury packaging](/blogs/moq-realities-luxury-packaging). To send a brief for a press kit box, the fastest route is to send product dimensions, a reference image for the finish direction, and the in-hand date.
