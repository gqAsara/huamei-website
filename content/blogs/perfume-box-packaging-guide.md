# Perfume box packaging: materials, structures, and specifications for fragrance brands

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 26 May 2026. Updated 26 May 2026.

Sonia Sun has produced perfume box packaging for fragrance and cosmetics brands from Huamei's factories in Henan, Zhejiang, Sichuan, and Guizhou since founding the company in 1992 — including custom rigid boxes, insert systems, and foil finishes for brands sold across Asia and in international markets.

Perfume box packaging is among the most specification-sensitive categories in luxury rigid-box manufacturing. The bottle is fragile, the brand identity typically rests on a small decorated face, and the unboxing moment is the first physical interaction the end buyer has with a fragrance SKU. Getting the box wrong — wrong board weight, wrong insert fit, wrong foil registration — is visible immediately, at the most expensive possible point in the customer journey.

This guide covers the materials, structures, surface finishes, and insert options used in custom perfume box packaging, and what a production brief needs to contain for a first custom run.

## What materials are used in perfume box packaging?

> Perfume box packaging uses 1.5–2.5 mm greyboard as the structural core, wrapped in specialty paper or fabric, with hot-foil stamping, emboss, or soft-touch laminate finishes. An internal flocked tray or foam insert holds the bottle upright and prevents movement in transit.

The greyboard core determines the box's weight, rigidity, and corner profile. For a standard single-bottle fragrance box in the 100–500 ml range, the standard board weight is 2.0 mm — the cosmetics-industry benchmark that balances a substantial feel-in-hand against manageable unit weight for retail display. A gift set containing two or more bottles — a perfume and travel size, or a fragrance and body care pairing — typically uses 2.5 mm board for the increased structural load.

The wrap material drives the visual register. Eighty paper substrates are available at Huamei's factories, ranging from uncoated naturals through to gloss-laminate coated stocks and specialty surfaces including linen, pearlescent, and embossed texture papers. Fabric wraps — cotton, silk-effect, and velvet — apply to collector and limited-edition SKUs where the surface tactility is a primary brand signal.

## What surface finishes are standard on fragrance packaging?

Fragrance packaging uses a narrower set of finishes than spirits or food gifting — the aesthetic register is typically restrained, with decoration concentrated on the lid face and spine rather than all-over.

The standard finishing stack on a luxury perfume box:

- **Hot-foil stamping:** the primary decorative technique on fragrance packaging. Seventeen curated colours are available in-house at Huamei. Gold and silver foil remain the most common choices for fragrance; platinum, champagne, and holographic foils appear on collector editions. Foil is applied in registration at ±0.1 mm — tighter than the industry-typical ±0.3 mm — so the foil edge aligns exactly with the emboss relief. [FSC](https://fsc.org/en)-certified paper substrates are available for fragrance brands with sustainable-packaging commitments.
- **Emboss and deboss:** three-dimensional relief on the lid, typically the brand wordmark or house monogram. Used in combination with foil to create a tactile–visual hierarchy: the buyer sees the foil first, then feels the emboss depth. See [/craft/hot-foil](/craft/hot-foil) and [/craft/emboss](/craft/emboss) for the technical specifications.
- **Soft-touch lamination:** matte velvet surface that photographs well in e-commerce and feels luxurious in hand. Common on mid-range fragrance retail boxes and travel-size packaging.
- **Gloss UV varnish (spot UV):** selective gloss accent on a matte-laminate background. Applied over the brand logo or a pattern element to create contrast without adding colour.

The critical specification for fragrance packaging is finish durability at the contact points — the box edge, the lid spine, and the top face where a bottle cap rests during unboxing. Soft-touch lamination and gloss lamination are both tested against scuffing and delamination at those contact zones before production approval.

## How is the insert designed for perfume packaging?

The insert design is where perfume packaging briefs most often go wrong. A loose bottle in a rigid box is worse than no box at all — it signals that the packaging was not designed for the product.

Three insert systems are standard for fragrance:

- **Flocked tray:** a thermoformed or die-cut tray covered in a fine flocked fabric (typically black, cream, or burgundy) that cradles the bottle base and holds it upright. The flock surface grips the bottle without scratching; the colour contrast between flock and wrap interior adds a reveal moment when the lid opens. This is the most common insert system for single-bottle fragrance.
- **Foam insert:** a cut foam block with a bottle-profile cavity. Common in transit-heavy or export applications where the priority is protection over reveal experience. Can be laminated in fabric for a better aesthetic.
- **Paperboard tray:** a rigid paperboard structure with a bottle well, typically the same wrap paper as the exterior. Lower cost; appropriate for mass-market or travel-retail fragrance at lower price points.

The bottle cavity dimensions must be confirmed against a physical bottle sample before the insert is cut. A tolerance of ±0.5 mm in the cavity is standard; tighter tolerances are achievable but increase sampling cycle time.

## What certifications apply to fragrance box packaging?

Huamei holds BSCI, CE, [EQS](https://www.iso.org/iso-9001-quality-management.html), FSC, and SGS certifications — each relevant to fragrance brand buyers assessing supplier compliance. BSCI covers social-responsibility auditing; FSC covers sustainable paper-sourcing chain of custody; SGS covers independent product-quality verification.

For fragrance brands selling into EU markets, BSCI and FSC documentation are increasingly required by retail buyers as conditions of supplier approval. ## What does a perfume box packaging brief need to contain?

A complete brief for a custom perfume box run at 200+ pieces MOQ:

- **Bottle dimensions:** height, maximum diameter, base profile, cap type. Physical sample preferred; technical drawing acceptable.
- **Target board weight:** 2.0 mm standard; 2.5 mm for gift sets.
- **Wrap paper or fabric selection:** from the substrate library, or a brand-specified material (lead time extension likely for non-stock materials).
- **Surface finish:** foil colour(s), emboss depth, lamination type, spot UV areas.
- **Insert type:** flocked tray, foam, or paperboard — and insert colour.
- **Closure type:** lid and base (most common), magnetic closure, or ribbon pull.
- **Quantity:** 200+ pieces; volume affects unit cost and allocates press time.

Sample lead time is 7–10 days from an approved brief to a production-representative physical sample. Production runs 15–20 days from the approved sample. The [Collgene](/volumes/collgene) packaging case study is an example of a cosmetics brand brief executed at Huamei's factories.
