# Drawer box packaging: structures, specifications, and when to choose it

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 18 May 2026. Updated 18 May 2026.

Sonia Sun has produced drawer box packaging at Huamei's Henan factory since founding the company in Zhengzhou in 1992 — including the [Oriental Memoirs](/volumes/oriental-memoirs) laser-cut gift set, a drawer format with a ribbon pull and a die-cut interior showcase used for premium gifting presentation. For the structural detail, see [drawer box vs. magnetic closure](/blogs/drawer-box-vs-magnetic-closure-luxury-packaging).

The drawer box is the oldest luxury gift-box format in continuous production. It appears in tea chest design from the eighteenth century, in jewellery trade packaging from the mid-nineteenth, and today in premium gifting at MOQ 200 and above. Its longevity comes from a combination of structural qualities no other rigid box format fully replicates: the controlled reveal of a sliding tray, the structural rigidity of a nested sleeve, and the visible premium signal of a ribbon tab even before the box is opened. The construction context for this sits in [custom luxury rigid box manufacturing](/blogs/custom-luxury-rigid-box-manufacturing), which walks through greyboard, wrapping, and assembly tolerances end-to-end.

## What is a drawer box?

> A drawer box is a rigid gift-box format in which a tray slides horizontally out of a fixed outer sleeve on a ribbon pull. The outer sleeve is typically 2.0 mm greyboard wrapped in printed paper; the inner tray sits 2–4 mm narrower on each side so it slides freely without rattling. Both sleeve and tray are hand-assembled with PVA adhesive.

The outer sleeve is a three-sided channel — open at one end — into which the inner tray sits. A ribbon, typically 6–12 mm wide, threads through a die-cut slot at the closed end of the tray so the recipient can pull the tray forward. The ribbon is the tactile cue that initiates the reveal: the resistance of the tray sliding against the sleeve creates a deliberate, unhurried opening moment that a flip-lid cannot reproduce.

Huamei carries ninety-nine structures on file, of which the drawer box appears in multiple variants: single-tray (one pull direction), double-tray (opens from either end), and two-tier (stacked trays for multi-SKU gift sets where each SKU occupies its own layer). The standard single-tray format is the most common at volumes below 2,000 pieces; the two-tier format appears more often in spirits and premium cosmetic sets at 500+ pieces.

## What board weight and structure does a drawer box require?

A luxury drawer box uses 2.0–2.5 mm greyboard for the outer sleeve and 1.5–2.0 mm for the inner tray — two different weights serving two different structural jobs.

The outer sleeve carries the full structural load: it holds the interior tray under compression during stacking, protects the print surface against rub and impact in transit, and presents the exterior decoration. The greyboard is wrapped in a 105–157 gsm coated or uncoated paper — depending on the surface-finish brief — and bonded with a food-safe PVA adhesive. At 2.0 mm, the sleeve wall is rigid enough to stack ten boxes without deformation under transit-grade compression testing. Huamei tests empty boxes at simulated stacking loads before production sign-off.

The inner tray runs lighter at 1.5–2.0 mm because its structural job is narrower: to carry the product weight and to slide cleanly against the sleeve interior without deformation. The tray is lined with a secondary paper — usually uncoated or fabric-covered — chosen to complement the outer sleeve's register.

At the ribbon slot, the die is cut with a rounded-corner punch to prevent the slot from tearing under repeated use. The ribbon is stitched or heat-sealed at the tray interior so the pull attachment does not fail before the box reaches its destination.

## What surface finishes work on a drawer box?

The outer sleeve of a drawer box supports the full range of [rigid box](/craft/rigid) surface finishes: four-colour offset print, [hot-foil stamping](/craft/hot-foil) in any of Huamei's seventeen curated foil colours, emboss, deboss, spot-UV, and both soft-touch and matte lamination.

The sleeve surface is the primary brand-communication canvas. Hot-foil to emboss registration on the sleeve is held to ±0.1 mm — tighter than the ±0.3 mm industry standard — so a brand mark that uses both foil and emboss reads as a single precise element rather than two overlapping ones.

The inner tray edge is visible when the drawer is partially open — typically the first interior surface the recipient sees. A contrasting liner colour or a secondary brand-colour fabric facing on the tray edge is a low-cost detail that reinforces the unboxing signal. The transition from the outer sleeve's print-and-foil to a quiet interior liner tells the hand that the product inside is held with care.

## When should a brand choose a drawer box over a magnetic-flap lid?

A drawer box outperforms a magnetic-flap lid when the product brief requires simultaneous reveal of multiple items, or when the brand register favours a horizontal opening motion over a vertical lift.

A magnetic-flap lid opens upward and reveals the product from above. The ribbon-pull drawer opens horizontally and reveals the product from the front. For gift sets where the brand identity is strongest from the front face — a row of bottles, a row of skincare SKUs, a cosmetic palette — the horizontal reveal frames the products as they would appear on a retail shelf, which is often a stronger first-impression moment than looking down into an open box.

For single-SKU products with a strong top-surface graphic, the magnetic-flap lid is typically the better choice. For multi-SKU sets or products where the product label is the primary brand communication, the drawer box positions the recipient at the right angle for the reveal.

The [Oriental Memoirs](/volumes/oriental-memoirs) drawer set uses a laser-cut window in the outer sleeve so the product inside is partially visible before the tray is pulled — a format that works on counter display as well as in a gifting context, because the window communicates product before the box is opened.

## How does drawer box production scale from sample to full run?

Drawer box samples run on the same 7–10 day timeline as other [rigid box](/craft/rigid) formats, with one additional variable: the ribbon slot punch. The die for the ribbon slot is fabricated in 1–2 days and is part of the sample tooling. If the ribbon spec changes — width, material, colour — after sample approval, the slot die may require adjustment before production lock.

Production runs complete in 15–20 days from sample sign-off at MOQ 200+. Hand-assembly — aligning the ribbon slot, threading the ribbon, and sealing the attachment — is the rate-limiting step for complex configurations. The two-tier double-drawer format adds approximately 2–3 days to the standard production window at 200-piece volumes.
