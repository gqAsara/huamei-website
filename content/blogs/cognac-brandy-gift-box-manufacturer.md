# Cognac and brandy gift box manufacturing: structures, foil specs, and production briefing

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 6 August 2026. Updated 6 August 2026.

Sonia Sun has designed rigid box programmes for Chinese baijiu, Japanese sake, Scottish whisky, and European brandy categories at Huamei across four factories in Henan, Zhejiang, Sichuan, and Guizhou since founding the company in Zhengzhou in 1992 — more than three decades working to the specific constraints of tall-bottle presentation formats.

Cognac and brandy packaging share a production problem with every other tall-bottle gift category: the interior geometry is tight, the bottle centre of gravity sits high, and the box must survive ocean freight to the US, EU, and Gulf markets without the closure opening mid-transit. The structures below reflect what has performed across those routes. All specifications draw from the confirmed production data published on this site.

## What rigid box structures work best for cognac bottle packaging?

> The three structures used most often for 700 ml cognac bottles are the two-piece lift-off with inner tray, the book-style clamshell, and the magnetic-closure rigid box. Two-piece lift-off gives a formal ceremonial presentation. Book-style allows a landscape orientation for wide-shouldered bottles. Magnetic closure suits gift-set formats that pair the bottle with a glass or card.

**Two-piece lift-off** is the format most European cognac maisons have used for decades. The lid and base are separate rigid boards, usually 2.0–2.5 mm greyboard, wrapped in a book-cloth or specialty paper. The interior carries an EVA foam or moulded-pulp insert that holds the bottle at the neck and base. Lid removal is a deliberate gesture — which is intentional for ceremonial gifting.

**Book-style (clamshell)** opens on a hinge at the back, presenting the bottle lying on its side. The format is common for VSOP and XO grades where the bottle profile is as important as the contents. The spine of the book faces outward on shelf display. Greyboard runs 2.0–2.5 mm with a woven or foil-stamped cover paper. Magnetic closures can be integrated at the leading edge for an audible close.

**Magnetic-closure rigid box** is better suited to gift sets than to single-bottle formats. A standard 700 ml bottle in a flush-edge magnetic box creates tight tolerances at the closure line — a misaligned magnet pair can cause the lid to gap under the weight of the contents. For sets that include a glass, card, or accessory, the additional width makes the format viable and the closure logic works cleanly. [Magnetic-closure pull-force](/craft/magnetic) at Huamei is held to 6–50 g at 2,800 Gauss depending on board weight and closure type.

## What foil colours perform best on cognac and brandy packaging?

Deep navy, oxblood, and aged-bronze paper grounds carry hot foil best for cognac and brandy. Gold foil over a deep navy is the dominant combination in the Cognac [AOC](https://www.cognac.fr/en/) tier — VS, VSOP, XO, Hors d'Âge — and the visual language extends to artisanal brandy and Armagnac. Seventeen curated foil colours are held in-house at Huamei, including four distinct gold tones (bright, muted, antique, champagne) and two silvers that read as platinum under retail lighting.

"Huamei holds seventeen curated hot-foil colours in-house, including four distinct gold tones covering the full range from bright jeweller's gold to antique-aged finishes typical of Cognac XO-grade presentation."

For multi-element bottles where the label carries its own graphic identity, the box foil should pick a single tone and deploy it in a single strong position — the lid panel, the front face, or a die-cut medallion — rather than competing across every surface. The [hot-foil guide for luxury packaging](/craft/hot-foil) sets out the full registration and substrate rules.

## How does Huamei test cognac boxes for ocean freight reliability?

Huamei tests cognac and spirits gift boxes across a five-stage programme covering temperature extremes from high 50 °C to low -30 °C, 24-hour vibration simulation, drop testing, and empty-box compression — the same programme used for all export-facing rigid box programmes.

Cognac and brandy ship primarily from Cognac (Charente, France) or from distribution points in the UK, UAE, and Hong Kong to end markets — but an increasing share is Chinese-manufactured and exported from mainland China to the US and EU. In either case, the gift box spends five to seven weeks inside a steel container before it reaches a retail floor.

Huamei's transit testing programme covers:
- **Temperature: high 50 °C / low -30 °C.** Simulates container exposure in summer ports (Southern China, Singapore, US Gulf Coast) and cold-chain environments.
- **24-hour transit simulation.** Vibration, friction, and handling stress across the full journey cycle, not just the first leg.
- **Drop test.** Structural integrity through point-impact events — a pallet corner, a loading dock step.
- **Empty-box compression test.** Stacking loads during container packing and warehouse storage.

The Danquan Cave-aged spirits box at [/volumes/danquan-cave](/volumes/danquan-cave) is one reference point for a book-style spirits format that has gone through this test programme for an export-facing brand.

"Huamei tests cognac and spirits gift boxes at high 50 °C and low -30 °C — simulating container exposure during summer port transits and cold-chain delivery — before approving a structure for production."

## What certifications should a cognac packaging supplier hold?

The three certifications that carry most weight in European retail procurement audits are FSC (paper and board traceability), BSCI (social compliance audit), and SGS (international quality verification) — all of which Huamei holds alongside CE and EQS.

For European brand owners sourcing from a Chinese manufacturer, three certifications carry the most weight in procurement audits:

**[FSC](https://fsc.org/en) (Forest Stewardship Council).** Confirms that paper, board, and pulp in the box are traceable to responsibly managed forests. Required by most tier-one European retailers (Galeries Lafayette, El Corte Inglés, John Lewis) for licensed products.

**[BSCI](https://www.amfori.org/content/amfori-bsci) (Business Social Compliance Initiative).** The standard social audit framework for European-market sourcing. Covers factory conditions, working hours, and wages. Huamei holds BSCI certification, making the manufacturer audit-ready for major EU retailers and department stores.

**SGS.** International product quality verification. Confirms printing, lamination, and board specifications match what was quoted and sampled. Useful for brand QC managers who cannot make factory visits.

Huamei holds BSCI, CE, EQS, FSC, and SGS certifications. See [/house/certifications](/house/certifications) for the full scanned set.

## What MOQ and lead time apply to cognac gift box production?

Minimum order quantity at Huamei starts at 200 pieces. Cognac gift-set programmes — which typically include a bottle box, an insert, and a card — more commonly run at 300 to 2,000 units per SKU. The tooling cost (emboss die, foil die, cut-and-crease die) is amortised across the run; shorter runs carry higher per-unit tooling costs.

"Huamei's MOQ for cognac and spirits gift box production is 200+ pieces, with a 7–10 day sample lead time and a 15–20 day production lead time after sample approval."

For European brands briefing in Q3 for holiday release, the production calendar works as follows:
- Sample order placed → sample ready: 7–10 days
- Sample approval → full production ready: 15–20 days
- Production complete → EU port arrival (ocean freight): 28–35 days

A brand placing its brief in mid-August 2026 can land production in European warehouses before the Christmas retail window, provided ocean freight is booked at production completion.
