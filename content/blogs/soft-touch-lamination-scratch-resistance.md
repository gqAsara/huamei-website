# Soft-touch lamination scratch resistance: what affects it and how to specify it

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 10 June 2026. Updated 10 June 2026.

Sonia Sun has specified and quality-checked lamination on luxury rigid box wrap papers at Huamei's factories in Henan, Zhejiang, Sichuan, and Guizhou since founding the company in 1992 — across cosmetic, spirits, and gifting formats where soft-touch lamination is among the most frequently specified and most frequently under-specified surface finishes.

Soft-touch lamination is the most popular surface finish in luxury rigid box packaging. Its velvet warmth, fingerprint resistance, and tactile contrast with foil elements make it a strong default choice for premium formats. But scratch resistance — how well the laminated surface holds up under transport, retail handling, and consumer use — varies substantially depending on film thickness, coating formula, substrate, and application conditions. A soft-touch box that arrives on a retail fixture with visible scratching undermines the premium signal the finish is there to create. This guide covers the technical variables that govern scratch resistance and how to specify them. For the broader lamination comparison, see [lamination types for luxury packaging](/blogs/luxury-packaging-lamination-types) and [soft-touch vs spot UV](/blogs/soft-touch-vs-spot-uv-when-each-is-right).

## What determines scratch resistance in soft-touch lamination on luxury packaging?

Soft-touch lamination scratch resistance is determined by film thickness (typically 18–28 micron for luxury packaging), the coating chemistry of the top layer, substrate surface smoothness, and post-lamination handling. Thicker film with a matte polyurethane top coat on a smooth coated base provides the best scratch resistance for luxury rigid box surfaces.

The soft-touch effect is produced by a film with a microscopically rough surface that scatters light and creates the characteristic matte, velvety feel. That surface texture is also the part of the film most exposed to abrasion — fingernails, edges of adjacent boxes in a shipper, or rubbing against tissue paper in a presentation box. The variables that determine how well that surface holds are within the control of the specification, not fixed by the material category.

"Soft-touch lamination film for luxury packaging runs 18–28 micron — thinner film below 15 micron shows visible scratch marks under repeated handling, while film above 30 micron adds cost without proportionate scratch resistance gain."

## What film thickness provides adequate scratch resistance for luxury packaging?

A 22–25 micron soft-touch lamination film provides adequate scratch resistance for luxury packaging production — below 18 micron the film lacks sufficient build to absorb surface abrasion without exposing the base print layer, and above 30 micron the additional thickness adds cost without proportionate resistance gain. The working range used in luxury packaging production is 18–28 micron.

For rigid box wrap paper — typically a coated art base of 120–157 gsm — 22–25 micron is the standard specification. This provides adequate scratch resistance for retail handling and transit in a corrugated shipper, while maintaining the tactile softness that distinguishes soft-touch from a standard matte laminate.

"For rigid box wrap paper at 120–157 gsm, a 22–25 micron soft-touch film provides adequate scratch resistance for retail handling and transit in a corrugated shipper without compromising tactile softness."

The [Collgene skincare case study](/volumes/collgene) demonstrates a skincare packaging programme where soft-touch lamination was used on a tuck-end carton format — the same scratch resistance principles apply to the rigid box wrap.

## How is soft-touch lamination scratch resistance tested?

Soft-touch lamination scratch resistance is tested using [ISO 1518-1](https://www.iso.org/standard/29235.html) scratch resistance protocols — a stylus drawn across the laminated surface at defined loads — and [ASTM D2197](https://www.astm.org/d2197-16.html) scrape adhesion tests, which measure whether the lamination film delaminates from the substrate under a scrape load. At the factory level, a practical fingernail scratch test is used for pre-production validation before formal testing is commissioned.

At the factory level, the practical test most commonly used before production sign-off is the fingernail scratch test: a fingernail drawn across the laminated surface at 45 degrees with moderate pressure. A well-specified soft-touch film shows no visible scratch at this load. If a scratch is visible, the film is under-specified for the handling conditions the box will encounter. This is a proxy test — not an ISO measurement — but it catches the most common specification failures before they enter production.

"Huamei applies soft-touch lamination as part of a transit-test protocol — laminated samples are subjected to vibration, drop, and friction cycles to confirm surface integrity before production sign-off, covering high 50°C, low −30°C, and 24-hour vibration conditions."

## How does soft-touch scratch resistance compare with gloss and matte laminates?

Soft-touch lamination sits between matte lamination and gloss lamination on the scratch resistance spectrum. Gloss lamination has the highest scratch resistance of the three because the smooth, high-build film surface resists abrasion without exposing the matte micro-texture. Standard matte lamination has a lower scratch resistance than soft-touch because it uses a thinner build and a less durable top-coat formula.

Soft-touch's improved scratch resistance over standard matte comes from the thicker film build and the polyurethane top-coat chemistry, which is more abrasion-resistant than the acrylic-based coatings used in standard matte films. The trade-off is cost: soft-touch film at 22–25 micron costs more per square metre than matte lamination at 12–15 micron.

"Soft-touch lamination applied over a smooth coated art paper base produces better scratch resistance than the same film applied over a textured or embossed wrap, because the contact area between the film and substrate is more uniform and the film adhesion is more consistent."

Applying [spot UV](/craft/spot-uv) panels over soft-touch lamination introduces a different surface finish where the UV-cured resin is highly scratch-resistant — the contrast between matte soft-touch and gloss UV panel is a design choice that also differentiates the scratch resistance of the two zones on the same surface.

## How should scratch resistance requirements be specified in a packaging brief?

A soft-touch lamination scratch resistance specification in a brief should state minimum film thickness (target 22–25 micron), the pass condition for a fingernail scratch test under moderate pressure, and whether transit validation is required before production sign-off — these three elements allow the manufacturer to set up, sample, and validate the surface before committing to the full production run. If the box will be sold through retail where surface-to-surface contact with adjacent units is unavoidable, specifying a retail-handling simulation test — boxes stacked without outer sleeves for 48 hours — before the production run is a reliable indicator of how the finish will perform on shelf.

The substrate matters equally. A soft-touch film of 22 micron applied over a 157 gsm coated art base performs differently from the same film applied over an 85 gsm uncoated textured paper: the textured surface creates variation in adhesion depth, which creates variation in scratch resistance across the sheet.

"A complete soft-touch lamination specification for a luxury rigid box brief includes film thickness (target 22–25 micron), a fingernail scratch test pass requirement, substrate specification (coated base preferred), and a transit validation test for any box sold through retail channels."
