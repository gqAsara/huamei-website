By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 2026-07-25. Updated 2026-07-25.

Sonia Sun has managed multi-process print production at Huamei since 1992 — three decades of aligning offset ink, foil, emboss, and laminate on the same surface without letting one process undermine another.

A luxury rigid box with both offset print and hot-foil stamping is asking two entirely different colour systems to coexist on the same substrate. Offset ink and metallic foil behave differently in production, respond differently to paper, and are specified in different technical languages. Getting both right on the first sample run is not complicated — but it requires a brief that treats the two processes separately and understands where they interact.

## How do you hold colour consistency across offset print and hot foil on a luxury rigid box?

Colour consistency across offset and hot foil requires separate specifications for each process: a Pantone reference matched to an offset ink mix, and a named foil colour from the manufacturer's foil book. The two are not interchangeable, and trying to match foil colour to an offset Pantone is a common brief error.

Offset printing uses four-colour process (CMYK) or Pantone spot colours mixed from ink in the press inking unit. [FOGRA](https://www.fogra.org/en/) PSO profiles standardise the colour output of offset press systems — the same profile logic that Huamei applies on the [Heidelberg](https://www.heidelberg.com/) and [KBA](https://www.koenig-bauer.com/) press lines. When a brand specifies a [Pantone](https://www.pantone.com/) PMS reference, the printer converts it to either a spot ink mix (more accurate, higher cost) or a CMYK approximation (less accurate, lower cost). For luxury packaging, spot-ink matching to the PMS reference is the correct choice.

Hot-foil colour is entirely separate. A metallic foil is a polyester film with a metallic or pigmented layer; it is not mixed from process inks and cannot be targeted against a Pantone reference. Instead, it is specified from a foil manufacturer's swatch book — a named foil code (e.g., "Gold 103" or "Silver Mirror 02") whose colour and reflectance are fixed by the film composition. Huamei's seventeen curated foil colours are in-house stock; they cover warm golds, cool silvers, bronze, rose-gold, and iridescent spectrums, and the physical swatch is what you spec against, not a Pantone equivalent.

## Why offset and foil interact at the paper substrate

The paper wrapped around the greyboard is the common substrate on which both processes land. The paper's surface — its gloss level, coating weight, and base colour — changes how both processes read to the eye.

A high-gloss coated art paper makes offset ink appear saturated and slightly darker than the same ink on an uncoated sheet. The same foil on a gloss sheet versus a matte uncoated sheet looks sharper on gloss (more reflective) and warmer/softer on matte. When a brief specifies both deep-colour offset print and a bright foil panel on the same exterior surface, the paper choice is a significant colour variable.

"The paper substrate changes how both offset ink and hot foil read to the eye — the same gold foil looks sharper on a gloss art base and warmer on a matte uncoated base. Specify the paper before finalising the foil code."

At Huamei, the standard sequence is: confirm paper → approve offset colour proof on that paper → confirm foil swatch on the same paper → run the combined sample. Running the offset proof on a different paper than the final production stock, then swapping paper before foil tooling, is the most common cause of sample-to-production colour shifts.

## Registration between offset and foil: the ±0.1 mm standard

Where offset print and hot foil meet on the same panel — a foil wordmark aligned to an offset-printed frame, or a foil panel that butts against an offset-printed background — registration is the specification that determines whether the result looks intentional or careless.

Huamei holds hot-foil-to-offset registration to ±0.1 mm on the Heidelberg and KBA press lines. The industry-typical tolerance for a foil-on-print application is ±0.3 mm. The difference is visible: at ±0.3 mm, a 2 mm foil frame around an offset element can show up to 15% of its width as a gap or overlap. At ±0.1 mm, the gap is imperceptible.

"Huamei holds hot-foil-to-emboss registration to ±0.1 mm — three times tighter than the industry-typical ±0.3 mm — on Heidelberg and KBA press lines."

The technical detail behind this: registration is maintained by consistent paper feed, press sheet tension, and die alignment. On the Heidelberg and KBA platforms, the sheet-registration system (side and front lays) is repeatable to sub-millimetre tolerance at production speed. What undermines registration is paper stretch from humidity changes between the offset pass and the foil pass — a real variable in humid production environments. Huamei's Henan factory controls press-room humidity for exactly this reason.

For more on registration, the article on [registered emboss-and-foil tolerance](/blogs/registered-emboss-foil-tolerance) covers ±0.1 mm in the context of a dual-process emboss and foil application.

## How to brief colour for a multi-pass luxury print run

A correct multi-pass colour brief specifies each element independently:

1. **Offset background or solid colour panels:** Pantone PMS reference + "spot ink match" instruction. Do not leave it to a CMYK approximation for a dominant brand colour.
2. **Foil panel or wordmark:** Named foil code from the manufacturer's swatch book. Specify "warm gold 103" or whichever code, not "gold Pantone 871." 
3. **Paper substrate:** Stock name or weight class. Confirm before offset proof.
4. **Registration requirement:** "Foil-to-offset ±0.1 mm" or whatever tolerance your brand requires. Stating it explicitly means the manufacturer knows it is being measured.
5. **Emboss / deboss (if any):** Specify the registration between the emboss and any overlying foil separately. The [emboss and hot foil technique page](/craft/emboss) covers how die design affects registration on a dual-pass run.
