# Magnetic box packaging: closure types, magnet specification, and structural variants

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 30 May 2026. Updated 30 May 2026.

Sonia Sun oversees magnetic closure engineering across Huamei's four factories in Henan, Zhejiang, Sichuan, and Guizhou, where the company has produced magnetic box packaging since founding in Zhengzhou in 1992 — across spirits, cosmetic, tea, gifting, and wellness sectors. Structural detail on rigid box construction is at [magnetic closure boxes](/craft/magnetic).

Magnetic box packaging covers a wider range of structures than the phrase suggests. The common thread is a neodymium magnet pair — one in the lid, one in the base wall — that closes the box without a latch, button, or visible hardware. The closure mechanism is inside the board sandwich, invisible from the outside. Beyond that shared principle, the structural variants, magnet grades, greyboard weights, and pull-force targets differ enough that a brief which specifies only "magnetic closure" is underspecified. This guide covers the variables that determine what the closure actually does.

## What is magnetic box packaging?

> Magnetic box packaging is a rigid box where neodymium magnets embedded in the greyboard create a self-closing lid or flap. Pull-force ranges from 6 g (soft guided close) to 50 g (firm snap) at 2,800 Gauss. Three structural variants — flip-top, clamshell, and drawer-with-magnet-latch — cover most luxury packaging briefs.

The magnet is a neodymium (NdFeB) disc or bar, typically N35 to N42 grade, recessed into the greyboard at a pre-cut position and sealed under a liner layer before wrapping. The polarity is set during insertion so that lid and base panels attract on the correct axis — vertical pull for a flip-top or clamshell, horizontal for a drawer latch. Huamei holds all magnetic box packaging to 2,800 Gauss as the production standard across the range.

Pull-force is the number that defines the closure experience. Huamei's range is 6–50 g at 2,800 Gauss. A 6–15 g pull produces a closure that guides the lid into position — the magnet assists without snapping; the user can interrupt the close midway without resistance. A 30–50 g pull produces a definite snap that holds the lid against light vibration or inversion. The correct pull-force depends on the product weight, the box dimensions, and the opening ritual: a gift box meant to be passed between hands before opening benefits from a secure closure; a retail display box opened dozens of times per day benefits from a lighter one.

## What structural variants exist in magnetic box packaging?

The three main structural variants handle the closure differently, and the right choice depends on the product form factor and the opening sequence the brand wants to design.

**Flip-top magnetic box.** A hinged lid connected to the base along one wall, with a magnet pair at the opposite (opening) edge. The lid lifts to approximately 120–170 degrees and is held open by the geometry of the hinge when fully extended. This structure is common for cosmetic sets, spirits accessories, and tea gift boxes. The [Hetao Wang](/volumes/hetaowang) open-face rigid format uses a similar planar lid geometry. Flip-top structures are efficient for display: the open box presents the product face-up and the lid rests against a vertical surface at retail.

**Clamshell magnetic box.** Two identical halves hinged at the base, both sides of equal depth. Magnets at the top closure edge draw the two halves together. Clamshell construction gives a symmetrical profile when closed and a symmetric opening experience — neither side is "lid" or "base" in the conventional sense. This is the structure used for products where the opening axis is the visual centre of the experience, and for presentation cases where the product rests in a tray inlay spanning both halves.

**Drawer with magnetic latch.** A pull-drawer box where a magnet embedded in the drawer front and a corresponding steel disc in the slipcase body hold the drawer closed during transit. The drawer pulls out on a single axis; the magnetic latch prevents the drawer from sliding open under its own weight. This structure suits products with a cylindrical or irregular cross-section that a hinged lid cannot accommodate. The [Yangshao Caitao](/volumes/yangshao) bottle-format box uses a drawer approach for the same reason — the product geometry dictates the opening axis.

## How does greyboard weight affect magnetic closure performance?

Greyboard weight affects pull-force, lid edge precision, and the weight-in-hand register of the finished box.

A 1.5 mm greyboard lid on a flip-top structure will carry a 2,800-Gauss magnet pair in the 10–20 g pull-force range cleanly. At 25 g and above, the board may flex slightly at the closure point under repeated opening if the structure geometry is wide. A 2.0 mm board adds rigidity that keeps the lid flat through repeated use cycles and allows pull-force up to 35 g without flexion. A 2.5 mm board is the specification for large-format or heavy-product boxes — 35–50 g pull-force range — where the combined board and product weight demand a closure that holds under vibration.

The greyboard weight also affects the wrapped lid edge. A thicker board produces a more defined edge profile at the closure point — relevant when the lid edge is a visible design element in the closed-box profile. For structures where the lid-to-base gap is a deliberate design feature, tighter greyboard tolerances produce a more consistent gap across a production run.

## What finishes are compatible with magnetic box packaging?

All standard rigid box finishes — offset print, hot-foil, emboss, deboss, spot UV, soft-touch laminate, and specialty paper wraps — are compatible with magnetic box packaging because the magnet mechanism is fully internal.

Huamei holds seventeen curated foil colours in-house. Hot-foil registration on magnetic box lid panels is held to ±0.1 mm — the same standard as non-magnetic rigid boxes. The magnet recess does not affect the wrap paper application or the surface decoration process. The finished exterior of a magnetic box is indistinguishable from a standard rigid box until the lid is closed and the snap is felt.

The one structural constraint: foil and emboss motifs that cross the closure edge must account for the lid overhang, typically 1–2 mm on each side of the closure line. A motif registered to the lid panel will land correctly on the lid; a motif intended to span lid and base must be specified as a split design. The [craft/magnetic](/craft/magnetic) page covers this in detail with structural diagrams.

## What certifications apply to magnetic box packaging for export?

For US and EU brands sourcing magnetic box packaging from China, the relevant certifications are [FSC](https://fsc.org/en) (paper and board sourcing), [BSCI](https://www.amfori.org/en/tools-and-services/amfori-bsci) (factory social compliance audit), and SGS (third-party quality inspection).

Huamei holds BSCI, CE, EQS, FSC, and SGS certifications. The neodymium magnets used in closures are sourced from verified suppliers and are not subject to separate certification under EU RoHS or REACH for packaging applications.
