# Custom champagne presentation box: structures, bottle protection, and surface treatments for sparkling wine brands

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 2 June 2026. Updated 2 June 2026.

Sonia Sun has produced premium bottle packaging across Huamei's four factories in Henan, Zhejiang, Sichuan, and Guizhou since founding the company in Zhengzhou in 1992 — including rigid box formats for baijiu, wine, and sparkling wine brands that ship to US and EU markets. Rigid box structures are at [rigid box structures](/craft/rigid); surface decoration including gold and metallic foil is at [hot-foil stamping](/craft/hot-foil).

A champagne presentation box carries a different structural brief from a standard gift box. The bottle is heavy, fragile, and sealed under pressure. The packaging must immobilise it against vibration across ocean freight, present it clearly when the lid opens, and project the occasion register — celebration, ceremony, gift-giving — through its surface treatment. These are three distinct engineering problems that the box must resolve simultaneously.

## What is a custom champagne presentation box?

> A custom champagne presentation box is a rigid packaging format built to hold one or two 750 ml bottles upright or angled, with a structured insert that immobilises the bottle at the neck and base. Board weight runs 2.0–3.0 mm; surface treatments range from soft-touch matte to full gold-foil stamping. MOQ from 200 pieces.

The standard format for a single-bottle champagne presentation box is a hinged or lift-off lid with a die-cut greyboard insert. The insert holds the bottle at the base (a circular well cut to the punt diameter), at the mid-body (a shaped cradle that prevents lateral movement), and at the neck (a die-cut collar or padded ring). Three contact points, calibrated to the bottle's specific geometry, produce a zero-movement result in freight.

## What board weight does a champagne presentation box require?

A champagne presentation box uses 2.5–3.0 mm greyboard — heavier than a standard gift box — because the bottle weighs 1.3–1.8 kg filled, and the base must carry that load without flex during stacking or handling.

A standard single-bottle champagne presentation box uses 2.5 mm greyboard with a die-cut tray insert that contacts the bottle at the base, mid-body, and neck — three contact points that eliminate movement during sea freight. The insert itself is typically cut from 3.0 mm greyboard, lined with velvet or EVA foam to prevent surface contact marks on the bottle.

For a two-bottle format, board weight typically steps up to 3.0 mm on the base and side panels, with a divider insert that separates and immobilises both bottles independently. The total assembled weight of the filled box is a structural input: if the shipping outer is also rigid board, the compression load from stacking three layers of filled boxes must not exceed the base's compression rating.

## What surface treatments suit a champagne presentation box?

Gold hot-foil on a dark ground is the dominant aesthetic in the champagne and sparkling wine category. The combination reads celebration in most global markets. Seventeen curated foil colours are held in-house at Huamei — from warm gold to cold silver to rose gold — applied at ±0.1 mm registration.

Hot-foil in gold on a champagne presentation box applies at ±0.1 mm registration, holding consistent across the lid face, side panel, and base wrap. At that tolerance, brand marks and decorative borders align precisely across all visible faces when the box is presented closed and open.

The typical surface construction for a champagne presentation box is: 2.5 mm greyboard base, coated in a matte-black or deep-navy 80-paper or art paper, with gold hot-foil on the lid face and a gold-foil border on the side panels. Soft-touch lamination over the outer paper adds a tactile surface that reads against the foil. A contrast satin ribbon, tucked under the lid edge, aids opening without handling the box surface.

For a worked example of how bottle-format rigid boxes handle glass-lined and metallic details, the [Wuliangye Premium Brew case study](/volumes/wuliangye-clamshell) shows a glass-lined clamshell format for a premium spirits bottle.

## How is a champagne bottle secured inside a presentation box?

The insert is the critical structural element. A die-cut insert for a 750 ml champagne bottle requires five dimensions: base punt diameter, base-to-shoulder height, maximum body diameter, shoulder taper angle, and neck diameter at the wire cage ring. From those five numbers, the insert geometry is cut on a flatbed die, with a tolerance of ±0.5 mm on the critical contact surfaces.

Velvet lining on the contact surfaces prevents glass contact marks and adds tactile warmth to the interior. EVA foam is used where the insert needs to absorb impact in a drop test; it compresses under load and recovers. The choice between velvet and EVA depends on the aesthetic of the interior and the freight risk profile.

Magnetic closure pull-force for a champagne box lid runs 6–50 g at 2,800 Gauss — adjustable by magnet count to match the lid weight of larger-format bottles. A 3.0 mm lid panel over a 750 ml bottle format typically requires two magnets at the mid-lid position to close firmly without slamming.

## What transit tests apply to champagne presentation packaging?

Champagne packaging that travels ocean freight to the US or EU must pass temperature and vibration testing before production approval.

Huamei runs transit-grade testing at high 50°C and low −30°C — simulating container sun exposure and cold-chain arrivals — alongside 24-hour transit vibration, drop testing, and empty-box compression. Transit-grade vibration testing for 24 hours on packaging confirms structural stability after stacking, vibration, and thermal cycling from −30°C to 50°C.
