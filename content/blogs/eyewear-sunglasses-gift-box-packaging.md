# Eyewear and sunglasses gift box packaging: hinged clamshell, velvet inserts, and foil specifications

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 2026-07-26. Updated 2026-07-26.

Sonia Sun has run the Huamei press floor since founding the company in Zhengzhou in 1992 — more than three decades building presentation structures for products where the unboxing moment is as deliberate as the product itself.

A pair of premium sunglasses or optical frames arrives in the customer's hands inside a box that signals the brand's intent before the tissue paper is lifted. The structure, the finish, and the insert material are each a decision — and each is available from a single manufacturer running ninety-nine rigid box structures across four factories in Henan, Zhejiang, Sichuan, and Guizhou.

## What rigid box structure works best for eyewear and sunglasses gift packaging?

A hinged clamshell rigid box with a magnetic closure and a contoured foam or velvet insert is the standard structure for eyewear gift packaging. The insert holds the frame securely, the magnetic closure keeps the box shut in transit, and the hinged lid allows single-handed opening.

The hinged clamshell is the dominant format for optical gift packaging for three functional reasons. First, it opens with one hand — the recipient lifts the lid and the product is immediately visible in its insert without having to set the box down and lift a separate lid. Second, the continuous hinge distributes stress across the full back panel of the box, which means the lid does not rack or warp under repeated opening and closing — relevant for a box a customer will keep and reuse. Third, the magnetic closure at the front of the lid creates the tactile signal that the contents are worth protecting: the slight resistance and soft click of a properly calibrated magnet is a better experience than a lid that simply rests in place.

"Huamei's hinged clamshell rigid boxes hold magnetic closure pull-force to 6–50 grams at 2,800 Gauss — calibrated to lid weight and board thickness so the closure functions correctly through the expected opening cycle count."

## Which insert material works for eyewear packaging

Velvet flocking and EVA foam are the two dominant insert materials for eyewear gift boxes, and they solve different problems.

**Velvet flocking** is applied directly to the contoured insert form. The surface is visually consistent — the same velvet nap reads across the entire insert regardless of the cut profile — and it photographs well against the eyewear product. Velvet flocking is the correct choice when the insert is likely to be visible in product photography or unboxing content, because the material integrates visually with the frame rather than reading as a foam block underneath it.

**EVA foam** is the structural choice when the primary function is transit protection. A contoured EVA foam insert cut to the specific frame geometry holds the temples in position, prevents the lenses from contacting the box walls, and absorbs the impact energy from corner drops. For eyewear with complex arm hinge geometry or wide temple spread, EVA allows tighter tolerance on the cutout than velvet-wrapped forms. See the [/craft/inserts](/craft/inserts) overview for how insert materials are specified and cut.

For a luxury retail gift box that will also be shipped direct to consumer, the practical answer is often a velvet-flocked insert with EVA foam beneath it: the velvet provides the visual register and the foam provides the transit protection. The two-layer insert is specified at the brief stage and cut to the same template.

"A velvet-flocked EVA foam insert for eyewear combines visual register — the same nap across the full insert surface — with the contour tolerance needed to hold a specific frame geometry in position through a corner drop test."

## Surface finishes and foil specifications for optical brands

Eyewear brands in the luxury and premium segments carry strong visual identities — the same colour standards and brand typographic weight that appear on retail fixtures must appear on the packaging. Hot-foil and offset print are both available, and the correct answer depends on whether the brand mark is a solid logotype or a detailed pattern.

Hot-foil stamping is the better process for a logomark that requires precise edge definition against a soft-touch or textured ground. Huamei holds [hot-foil](/craft/hot-foil) registration to ±0.1 mm — three times tighter than the industry-typical ±0.3 mm — which matters when a brand wordmark at small point sizes is stamped in gold or silver over a dark base paper. Seventeen curated foil colours are available in-house; matte gold, gloss silver, and rose gold are the most common for optical-segment packaging.

Soft-touch lamination is the dominant base finish for premium eyewear boxes. The tactile quality — the slight resistance as the finger moves across the panel — is consistent with the brand register of eyewear at this price point. It is applied before foil decoration and before any spot-UV highlights; the sequencing is managed at the press stage.

"Soft-touch lamination applied to a rigid box wrapping paper creates a matte tactile surface with a coefficient of friction that makes the box feel heavier and more considered than its actual greyboard weight suggests."

## Transit specifications for eyewear gift boxes

Eyewear gift boxes present a specific transit challenge: the frame, often the most expensive component in the parcel, must not contact the box walls during the drop and vibration events that occur in normal courier and parcel logistics. The transit-grade quality programme at Huamei covers the full range of events — high 50 °C / low -30 °C temperature cycling, 24-hour vibration simulation, drop testing, aging, and empty-box compression — and the insert is tested loaded with the specific product weight and geometry, not as an empty cavity.

For brands shipping eyewear direct to consumer via courier, the master carton specification is as relevant as the individual gift box. A master carton of twelve gift boxes behaves as a unit during parcel sortation; the individual box insert must hold the frame correctly even when the master carton has been stacked, dropped, and repositioned multiple times. Brands briefing gift box packaging for DTC eyewear should specify courier-transit testing alongside the retail-presentation spec.

Production runs against [ISO 9001:2015](https://www.iso.org/iso-9001-quality-management.html) on the [Heidelberg](https://www.heidelberg.com/en/) and [KBA](https://www.kba.com/en/) press floor in Henan. The Huamei quality record for a gift box includes insert tolerance measurements and a sample drop-test result, available on request as part of the pre-shipment sign-off package.

## Briefing an eyewear gift box

A complete brief for an eyewear rigid box includes: the specific frame model (or a representative frame with the widest temple spread in the range), the target brand colours with Pantone references, the preferred insert material (velvet, EVA, or layered), the closure preference (magnetic or soft-close), the required foil colour if hot-foil is specified, and the destination port and freight terms. MOQ is 200 pieces.

The shortest path to a sample is to use an existing structure from Huamei's ninety-nine on-file forms and modify the insert geometry to the specific frame. Custom structures require a tooling brief and add to the sample timeline.
