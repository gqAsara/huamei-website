# Packaging manufacturer USA vs China: cost, lead time, certification, and when each is right

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 1 June 2026. Updated 1 June 2026.

Sonia Sun has produced luxury rigid box packaging for brands shipping into US and European markets since founding Huamei in Zhengzhou in 1992 — including transit-grade testing, [BSCI](https://www.amfori.org/en/tools-and-services/amfori-bsci) social audits, and FSC chain-of-custody certification at scale. Structural options are at [rigid box structures](/craft/rigid). Current certification documentation is at [/house/certifications](/house/certifications).

US brands sourcing luxury packaging in 2026 are working through a changed cost environment: revised tariff schedules on Chinese paper-based packaging have pushed landed cost upward, and domestic alternatives have matured. The question of "US vs China" is now a genuine procurement decision rather than a reflexive choice in either direction. This guide compares the two on the variables that drive that decision.

## Should you source luxury packaging from a US or Chinese manufacturer?

> US manufacturers offer shorter lead times (5–10 days) and no tariff exposure, but carry higher per-unit costs, shallower structure libraries, and limited hot-foil and emboss capability. Chinese manufacturers offer deeper craft capability, 99+ structures on file, and a per-unit cost advantage that holds even after 2026 tariff adjustments — for orders of 200+ pieces with 15–20 day production lead times.

The answer depends on three things: the surface decoration and structure complexity the brand requires; the order volume; and whether the lead time can absorb a 15–20 day production cycle plus ocean freight. If the answer to the last question is no, the correct path is to fix the planning calendar, not to switch suppliers.

## How do US and Chinese packaging manufacturers compare on cost and MOQ?

US luxury rigid box manufacturers typically carry MOQs of 500–1,000 pieces for custom structure and surface decoration work. Per-unit cost at 500 pieces from a US specialist runs 3–5× the equivalent from a Chinese factory with the same certification profile. The cost difference narrows at 2,000+ pieces, but the gap does not close.

Chinese rigid box manufacturers operate at MOQ floors starting at 200 pieces for complex rigid structures with hot-foil and emboss. At 200 pieces, a Chinese manufacturer produces a specification that a US manufacturer would price at a 500-piece minimum — and for significantly less per unit.

The 2026 tariff schedule on paper-based packaging from China (HTS Chapter 48) adds landed cost above the FOB price. Under current rates, the tariff-adjusted landed cost per unit for a mid-specification rigid box remains below the equivalent US domestic price for order sizes above 500 pieces. The break-even point shifts for simpler structures at lower volumes.

[ICC Incoterms](https://iccwbo.org/business-solutions/incoterms-rules/) govern the price basis on which Chinese packaging is quoted to US buyers. FOB Ningbo or FOB Shanghai is the standard departure point; the buyer arranges freight and insurance from that point. CIF (cost, insurance, freight) to a US port is available for buyers who prefer a delivered price. The tariff is assessed on the customs value at US port of entry regardless of Incoterms.

## What are the lead time differences between US and China packaging?

US manufacturers ship domestic orders in 5–10 business days for stock structures and 15–25 business days for custom. No freight time is added for US delivery.

Chinese manufacturers produce custom rigid box samples in 7–10 days and production runs in 15–20 days. Ocean freight from Ningbo or Shanghai to a US West Coast port adds 14–18 days; to a US East Coast port adds 25–30 days. Air freight to any US gateway takes 3–5 days and approximately doubles the freight cost per cubic metre.

The practical planning implication: a brand that locks its packaging brief 6–8 weeks ahead of its product launch date can reliably source from China, including freight. A brand that locks its brief 3 weeks before launch needs a domestic supplier or air freight — and should treat that lead time constraint as a planning failure to fix on the next cycle, not a structural reason to shift all sourcing.

Seasonal launch calendars — Chinese New Year, Christmas, Diwali, Valentine's Day — have known deadlines. A factory that has produced those programmes since 1992 plans those cycles forward from the retail ship date.

## How do certification and audit-readiness compare between US and Chinese factories?

Certification depth varies significantly between individual manufacturers in both geographies, so the comparison is factory-by-factory rather than country-wide. However, the international audit framework was built for Chinese export factories and is more uniformly deployed there than in the US domestic market.

[BSCI](https://www.amfori.org/en/tools-and-services/amfori-bsci) social compliance audits, FSC chain-of-custody certification, and SGS inspection are the three certifications European retail buyers require as a condition of first order. A Chinese factory competing for European retail packaging has structural incentive to maintain these certifications. A US domestic manufacturer supplying US brands has less exposure to European retailer audit requirements and may not hold the same documentation.

Huamei holds BSCI, CE, EQS, FSC, and SGS certifications. The factories run on >80% green energy, primarily solar generation. Transit-grade testing covers high 50 °C, low −30 °C, 24-hour vibration, drop, aging, and empty-box compression — the full suite that international logistics scenarios require. [Collgene's skincare packaging](/volumes/collgene) — produced at Huamei and sold into international channels — illustrates the certification stack in practice.

## When does China remain the better sourcing choice despite tariffs?

China is the correct sourcing choice when the packaging specification requires:

**Craft depth not available domestically.** Hot-foil stamping at ±0.1 mm registration on 2.0 mm greyboard, registered emboss-to-foil, seventeen curated foil colours in-house, eighty paper substrates on file — this depth of craft capability does not exist at scale in US domestic production. Brands specifying premium rigid box packaging with complex surface decoration have no domestic alternative at equivalent quality and MOQ.

**Structure variety.** Ninety-nine structures on file at Huamei — including bespoke engineering for bottle silhouettes, multi-compartment sets, and drawer-sleeve combinations. A US domestic supplier can handle lift-off lid and magnetic closure; the structural range stops there for most.

**Volume above 500 pieces.** At 500 pieces and above, the per-unit cost advantage of Chinese production holds even after tariff. The absolute tariff cost is a fixed percentage of the FOB price; the per-unit FOB price advantage grows with specification complexity.

**Long-term partnership risk.** A factory founded in 1992 with four facilities across Henan, Zhejiang, Sichuan, and Guizhou has the capacity depth to absorb demand surges, the tooling library to run repeat seasonal programmes, and the institutional memory to maintain consistency across production runs years apart. That stability is a supply-chain risk asset that domestic alternatives with shorter operating histories cannot match.
