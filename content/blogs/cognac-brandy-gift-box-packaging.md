# Cognac and brandy gift box packaging: bottle formats, structure options, and surface treatment

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 7 August 2026. Updated 7 August 2026.

Sonia Sun has specified tall-format spirits gift boxes at Huamei's factories in Henan, Zhejiang, Sichuan, and Guizhou since 1992 — including rigid presentation boxes for baijiu formats that share the tall-bottle geometry, dark-ground palette, and foil-heavy decoration of the cognac category.

Cognac packaging carries a specific set of category expectations: a tall box that matches a tall bottle, a dark ground that reads as premium on a shelf, and surface decoration in warm metallic foil that signals the appellation's age-grade. Those expectations are not decorative conventions — they are purchasing signals. A 700ml bottle of VS cognac presented in a box that reads like a generic spirits format will underperform the same liquid in a box that reads like a house with provenance, quality, and occasion value. The specification question for a brand commissioning cognac gift packaging is how precisely those category signals are achieved in the physical object.

## What makes cognac gift box packaging different from other spirits packaging?

Cognac gift box packaging is typically taller and narrower than whisky or baijiu formats: a standard 700ml cognac tall-shoulder bottle runs 330–360 mm in height, requiring a box interior of at least 345 mm with a 100–130 mm diameter cradle opening. The dark-ground visual register and registered foil-on-emboss decoration that define the category require hot-foil pressed to ±0.1 mm registration against the sculpted relief die.

The tall-format dimension distinguishes cognac packaging from most other spirits categories. A 700ml single-malt whisky typically runs 300–320 mm; a standard 500ml baijiu bottle runs 250–310 mm. The cognac tall-shoulder bottle — the silhouette associated with major [Cognac AOC](https://www.bnic.fr/en/) houses — requires a box substantially taller and narrower in footprint than a standard spirits format. That dimensional combination affects the greyboard specification: a tall narrow box without corner reinforcement is structurally weaker than a square format of similar height, and the board weight must account for it. Huamei's ninety-nine on-file structures include tall-format single-bottle [rigid boxes](/craft/rigid) dimensioned for this height range; a brief naming the bottle dimensions generates a structure recommendation within two business days.

"A 700ml cognac tall-shoulder bottle typically requires a box interior of at least 345 mm in height with a 100–130 mm diameter cradle opening — taller and narrower than most whisky or spirits formats, which affects the greyboard weight and corner reinforcement specification."

## What rigid box structures work for cognac and brandy gifting?

Three structures address most cognac and brandy gift packaging briefs: the two-piece lift-off rigid box, the magnetic closure rigid box, and the clamshell hinged box.

The two-piece lift-off [rigid box](/craft/rigid) is the most widely specified format for single-bottle cognac presentation. The base holds the bottle in a die-cut EVA or card cradle; the lid lifts off to reveal the bottle upright. The lid is the primary branding surface — [hot-foil](/craft/hot-foil) on a dark paper ground, with an embossed brand mark registered to the foil. This structure is appropriate for a premium presentation format where the bottle is the first thing a recipient sees on opening.

The [magnetic closure rigid box](/craft/magnetic) suits multi-piece gifting — a bottle plus miniatures, or a bottle alongside glassware or confectionery. The front flap opens on embedded magnets rather than lifting a full lid; the reveal is horizontal rather than vertical. Pull-force on Huamei's magnetic closures ranges from 6 to 50 g at 2,800 Gauss. For a cognac set where the flap opens to display a bottle and two snifters in a moulded card tray, a pull-force of 25–40 g is typical — firm enough to hold a weighted interior without sagging.

The clamshell hinged box suits a display-forward format where the box remains open at retail. It is more complex to assemble and costs more per unit than lift-off or magnetic formats; it belongs in the brief when the box is as much of the display as the bottle.

## What decoration anchors the cognac visual register?

The cognac visual language is anchored to three elements: a dark colour ground — black, deep burgundy, or navy; warm metallic hot-foil in gold, copper, or aged bronze; and a brand mark or coat-of-arms in registered foil-on-emboss. The result should read as heritage, provenance, and age — not as contemporary minimal.

Huamei holds seventeen curated [hot-foil colours](/craft/hot-foil) in-house. The gold palette spans champagne through deep antique gold; the copper range covers bright red-copper through aged bronze. Hot-foil is pressed at 120–160 °C with registration held to ±0.1 mm against any sculpted relief element on the same surface. A brand mark rendered in foil without the emboss is flat on the page; the same mark in registered foil-on-emboss reads as dimensional and is visible from an angle — the specification that separates a premium from a standard cognac gift box.

"Hot-foil-to-emboss registration at Huamei is held to ±0.1 mm — the tolerance required for a cognac brand mark to read as dimensional when viewed at an angle, rather than flat against the paper ground."

## How are cognac bottles supported in the interior?

The interior of a tall cognac gift box typically supports the bottle in one of three ways: a die-cut card cradle, a die-cut EVA foam insert, or a vacuum-formed plastic tray.

A die-cut card cradle — two or three card panels die-cut and interlocked to create a bottom support and a neck collar — is the lowest-cost option and consistent with [FSC](https://fsc.org/en)-certified, fully recyclable construction. An EVA foam insert is cut to the exact bottle profile with a 1–2 mm tolerance; it cushions better than card during transit but adds a plastic-derived component. A vacuum-formed tray offers the tightest fit and the clearest visual presentation but requires its own tooling, which adds to the upfront cost.

For cognac gift packaging targeting European retail under current sustainability audit expectations, the card cradle is the most defensible choice — it contributes to the overall recyclability of the pack. For gifting sets where the interior contains multiple items at different heights, an EVA insert is more practical because it can be specified per compartment.
