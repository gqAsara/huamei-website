# Paper gift box: substrates, finishes, and how paper choice shapes a packaging brief

By Sonia Sun, Founder, Huamei 華美 — since 1992. Published 26 May 2026. Updated 26 May 2026.

Sonia Sun has selected paper substrates for custom gift boxes at Huamei's factories in Henan, Zhejiang, Sichuan, and Guizhou since 1992 — specifying from a library of eighty papers across coated, uncoated, specialty surface, and FSC-certified stocks for spirits, cosmetics, and consumer gifting brands.

Paper is the most visible component of a gift box and the least specified. Most packaging briefs arrive with a clear board weight, a clear structural format, and a clear decoration intent — but no paper selection. The paper choice is left to the factory, which defaults to the most common substrate in that price tier. The result is packaging that performs but does not feel deliberate.

This guide explains how to think about paper selection for a custom gift box, what the primary substrate categories are, how paper interacts with surface finishes, and what to specify in a brief.

## What paper is used for gift boxes?

> Gift boxes use specialty wrapping papers in 80–160 gsm applied over a greyboard or paperboard core. Common substrates include coated art paper for sharp print reproduction, uncoated naturals for an editorial texture, pearlescent stocks for a metallic sheen without foil, and linen or felt-effect surfaces for a tactile brand signal.

The paper on a rigid gift box is a wrap — not a structural component. It is applied over the greyboard core (1.5–3.0 mm) with an adhesive layer, covering the exterior face and turning in to cover the interior walls. The paper's gsm (grams per square metre) determines fold quality at the corner, print sharpness, and surface durability. The typical wrap paper range is 80–160 gsm; papers below 80 gsm tend to show greyboard texture through the surface, and papers above 160 gsm are stiff enough to crack at corner turns.

Eighty paper substrates are on file at Huamei's factories, covering the full commercial range. The selection covers Heidelberg and [KBA](https://www.koenig-bauer.com/en/)-press-compatible stocks for four-colour process printing, as well as specialty surfaces that are pre-printed at the paper mill and applied without additional press work.

## What are the main paper substrate categories for gift boxes?

Paper substrates divide into four practical categories for gift box wrapping.

**Coated art paper** is the standard for gift boxes where four-colour process print is the primary decoration. The coating — gloss or matte — creates a smooth, receptive surface for [Heidelberg](https://www.heidelberg.com/) offset printing; colour saturation and dot gain are both more predictable than on uncoated stock. Gloss-coated art paper is common in food gifting and consumer goods; matte-coated art paper is the default in cosmetics and spirits where a satin finish reads as more elevated.

**Uncoated natural papers** — kraft, newsprint-texture, and laid-finish stocks — give a gift box an editorial, craft-oriented register. Print adhesion on uncoated stock requires slightly higher ink weight, and colour reproduction is less saturated than on coated stock; this is often intentional. Brands using uncoated papers are typically choosing the material as a brand signal rather than a neutral substrate. For sustainability-positioned packaging, uncoated stocks pair naturally with [FSC](https://fsc.org/en) certification and a visible paper-grain surface that communicates natural-material provenance.

**Specialty surface papers** — pearlescent, linen-texture, felt-effect, and metallic-base stocks — are pre-finished at the paper mill. They arrive at the factory already carrying the surface effect; the wrap step bonds them to greyboard without additional press decoration. Pearlescent papers give a foil-adjacent sheen without hot-foil stamping; linen and felt-effect stocks deliver a tactile brand signal that soft-touch laminate cannot replicate because the texture comes from the paper's formation rather than a surface coating.

**FSC-certified papers** are available across all four categories above. An [FSC](https://fsc.org/en) chain-of-custody certificate verifies that the paper fibres come from a responsibly managed forest source. For EU and US retail buyers with sustainable-packaging procurement policies, FSC certification on the wrap paper is often a condition of supplier approval, separate from the structural board certification. Huamei holds FSC certification covering both wrap papers and greyboard core.

## How does paper selection interact with surface finishes?

Paper choice constrains the available surface finishes — or enables specific finishes that would not work on a default substrate.

- **Hot-foil stamping:** works across all four paper categories. On coated stock, foil adhesion is reliable at the standard temperature and pressure settings. On uncoated stock, the foil interface is slightly different because the surface absorbs heat differently; the finish is achievable but requires a press trial for any new paper-foil combination. On specialty surface papers (particularly pearlescent and metallic-base), foil-on-foil finishes are possible and are a common collector-edition technique in spirits packaging. See [/craft/emboss](/craft/emboss) for the registered foil-to-emboss specification.
- **Soft-touch lamination:** applied over coated art paper to create a matte velvet surface. Soft-touch laminate over uncoated stock is unusual — the uncoated texture is already the desired aesthetic, and laminating over it neutralises the paper's natural hand-feel. Over specialty surface papers, soft-touch laminate typically reduces the surface effect (flattening pearlescent sheen, for example) and is rarely specified.
- **Emboss:** depth on the finished box is a function of both the greyboard core weight and the paper's gsm and formation. Coated art paper at 120–140 gsm over 2.0 mm greyboard gives a clean 0.8–1.0 mm emboss depth. Specialty surface papers — particularly linen and felt-effect — can distort at the emboss peak if the formation is uneven; a press trial confirms emboss quality before production.

## What paper finishes work for spirits and cosmetics gift boxes?

Spirits gift boxes — such as the [Yangshao](/volumes/yangshao) and [Hetao Wang](/volumes/hetaowang) presentation cases — typically use matte-coated art paper or a specialty surface with gold or silver foil, because the spirits category values a restrained, craft-adjacent aesthetic. The paper is usually dark — deep navy, forest green, or black — to set the foil contrast. Uncoated naturals appear in heritage-positioned spirits where the brand narrative is artisanal provenance.

Cosmetics gift boxes trend toward soft-touch laminate over coated art paper, with a lighter colour field (cream, blush, sage, grey) and emboss or deboss for the brand mark. Pearlescent and metallic-base papers appear in seasonal editions and gift sets where the unboxing moment carries additional visual weight. For the cosmetics sector, see [/industry/cosmetic](/industry/cosmetic) and [/craft/rigid](/craft/rigid) for the full rigid-box structure guide.

## How do I specify paper in a custom gift box brief?

The most useful paper specification in a brief is a reference sample — a swatch, a competitive pack, or a product description with a visible grain and surface direction. Abstract descriptions ("matte natural" or "luxury feel") are interpreted differently by different factories; a physical or image reference reduces the sampling cycle.

If no reference is available, specify by category (coated / uncoated / pearlescent) and by intended decoration: "matte-coated art paper for soft-touch laminate with gold foil" reads unambiguously. Colour is confirmed at the press-proof stage, not at the paper-selection stage.
